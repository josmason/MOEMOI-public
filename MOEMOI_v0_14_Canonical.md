## F1: The Principle

### F1.1 Master Claim (v1.3)
> **Core.** Nature *appears* to be optimality: across all known time, intelligence has trended toward the most optimal and ever more optimal, and it does so by running one process (the Intelligence Process) whose execution-quality shapes the outcomes any form of intelligence achieves.
> **Epistemic status.** Observed across known time; forecast to continue, with the rate of optimality-gain itself rising (recursive self-improvement: C2/C3); that rate's behaviour at very high values is unknowable at our current level of intelligence. Held as a **working assumption** ("solve for x": be/run/execute most optimally and ever more optimally) until evidence shows otherwise.
> **Execution → outcome.** The better a form executes each substep (quantity × quality), the more it shifts the odds toward the most optimal and ever more optimal outcome **within its constraints** (environment, interactions, everything it exists within). Good execution shifts odds; it does not guarantee a result (chance and other agents also act).
> **Sub-claims (subordinate).** Reducing **unnecessary** suffering is a **byproduct**, not the goal; MOEMOI is a distinct body of knowledge synthesising scattered disciplines around the single axis of optimality (in humans: self-life mastery); a dedicated vernacular is beneficial.

**Commentary: the treadmill, explained and directed** `[held fold: B232.2]`: MOEMOI offers a structural account of hedonic adaptation: the gap re-opens because the agent's model of the most optimal and ever-more-optimal state updates; which is correct IP (Intelligence Process) operation, not a failure. The prescription is not to eliminate the gap-reopening (that would be a static, non-optimising system) but to distinguish which re-opened gaps are worth chasing (generative voids) from which are anxiety-driven or conditional (deficit voids), and to run the IP on the former.

# PART A: THE INTELLIGENCE PROCESS (the spine)

## A0: Overview
The Intelligence Process (IP) is the one loop run by every **self-modelling information-processing system** to move from its current state toward a more optimal one, recursively. `[NEW 2 Jun: scope corrected, red-team RT1]` The earlier wording "every form of intelligence runs" overstated it and contradicted Part C, which concedes that pre-biological and supra-human rungs show only *analogical* complexification, not a genuine IP. The precise claim: **IP-strength rises with a system's capacity to model itself and its environment** (Define needs a modeller); sub-modelling systems (atoms, molecules) complexify on the entropy gradient but do not "run the IP." **Disproof criterion `[NEW 2 Jun]`:** a self-modelling system that reliably improves its own outcomes *without* any sense → understand → prepare → optimise structure would falsify the universality. *(Claim C3, restated to be falsifiable; resolves the A0↔Part C contradiction.)* **Person-independence status `[NEW 5 Jun fold]`** (OEQ-12): the universality claims (C3 here, C51 in E.1) read as **structurally separable (audited) but functionally unproven pending n≥2**: MOEMOI's structure is person-independent by audit, but that independence is *demonstrated, not yet empirically confirmed across subjects.* **Implicit vs deliberate IP (universality stratification) `[NEW v0.25 1 Aug]`** (NX-B30221-1; Trukovich 2025 R2R framework — Biosystems): The universality claim has two precision tiers that must not be conflated. ### A1.1 Perception quality: dualistic, monistic, and the cessation limit `[NEW: corrected]`

- **Beliefs are evidence-weighted.** A belief's strength = a function of its evidence, scored as **Quantity × Quality** (SE/ML/Qty×Qlty). Beliefs carry trackable properties (source, confidence, evidence-for/against, last-updated): the belief data structure (detailed in the item schema, A3). The aggregate of all beliefs = one's **Aggregate Reality** (total subjective model of the world). *(Claim C37.)*
- **The inference engine.** Bayesian updating (revise belief strength as evidence arrives) + decision theory (choose under uncertainty by expected value) + **fallacy-avoidance** (the known bias/fallacy list is screened against here). The wisdom criteria for decisions live here too: integrate across domains, balance values/stakeholders/timeframes, handle uncertainty, aim at flourishing not mere correctness, self-correct.
- **Motivated Understand evasion (self-deception). `[NEW v0.18]`** When accurate Perceive produces an uncomfortable signal, the agent may avoid running Understand on it — finding reasons to dismiss, minimise, or postpone processing the signal rather than actually working with it (Trivers 2011; Mele 2001). This is distinct from ordinary bias or fallacy: the input is not distorted, the reasoning capacity is intact, but the processing is obstructed by motivated avoidance. The check: *"Am I avoiding processing this Perceive signal because the conclusion would be uncomfortable?"* If the answer is yes, proceed with Understand anyway — the avoidance is itself diagnostic (it indicates high-relevance input the agent's model has not yet integrated). *(Claim C109. See also C130: motivated selection at the Decide substep, the downstream parallel where Understand has run correctly but the comfortable option is selected rather than the evidence-supported one.)* `[NEW v0.18 cross-ref 24 Jul]`
- **U2: Understand Sequencing Check `[NEW v0.18]`** Before processing the most accessible or comfortable evidence, identify the most uncomfortable implication of this gap — the interpretation you most want to be false. Process that interpretation first, before the main Understand pass. Rationale: the natural Understand sequence is biased toward the most accessible or least threatening material; reversing the sequence detects motivated evasion (C109) at the earliest point. This is a one-question prompt: *"What is the implication of this situation I most want to avoid being true?"* Run Understand on that first. *PLOS field:* `u2_sequencing_check` boolean (daily, for significant Understand tasks; routine retrieval is exempt).
- **High approach motivation (BAS activation) raises Understand bypass risk (C134). `[NEW v0.22 1 Aug]`** Positive anticipatory affect — the motivational state generated by a salient reward signal, labelled BAS (Behavioural Activation System) after Gray 1982 — systematically increases the probability that the Understand substep is abbreviated or skipped before Define and Decide. Four convergent mechanisms: (1) *Attentional narrowing* (Harmon-Jones 2012): BAS activation narrows attention toward the reward target and away from peripheral evidence, reducing the evidential base available to Understand; (2) *Appraisal-tendency* (Lerner and Keltner 2001): positive anticipatory states produce certainty appraisals, reducing perceived need to Understand further before acting; (3) *Affect heuristic substitution* (Slovic 2002): when a target feels good, positive affect substitutes for probabilistic Understand output, producing confident judgements without the work; (4) *Regulatory focus* (Higgins 1997): promotion-focused states (BAS-proximate) favour speed and approach over accuracy and caution. *Practical signal:* if the practitioner notices that a gap-closing action feels obviously correct and the Understand pass was brief — especially when the opportunity is time-pressured or rewards are salient — that conjunction is a C134 flag. The U2 Sequencing Check is the first-line intervention: the most uncomfortable implication of the situation is precisely the evidence BAS motivation is most likely to suppress. *(C134, ADOPTED ~0.80; 4-source consilience; IP-specific operationalisation is a MOEMOI extension not directly tested.)*

- **R1: Priming Scan (Retrieve substep practice) `[NEW v0.18 practice bundle 23 Jul]`** Before any significant planning, decision, or synthesis session, spend 2-4 minutes attempting free recall first: what do you already know about this topic, what have you previously concluded, what is your current model? - **Transform (cross-domain structural borrowing). `[NEW v0.18]`** Convert retrieved material into novel structures by mapping relational patterns from one domain onto another. *OC: a new relational or structural mapping has been produced that could not be derived from Retrieve alone — the output has a different form from the input.* Primary mechanism: identify the relational structure in a known domain (source), ask whether that structure maps onto the target domain (the current void), and import the mapping while testing which features transfer and which do not. *Grounding:* bisociation — two previously unconnected knowledge matrices producing a novel pattern at their intersection (Koestler 1964); structural mapping — analogies work by mapping relational structure, not surface features (Gentner 1983); analogy as the core of all high-level cognition (Hofstadter 1979, 1985). **Test-don't-inherit at the Transform level:** an analogy that maps relational structure is a valid Transform move; one that imports untested assumptions from the source domain is a failure mode — map the structure, test the assumptions. *Activation:* Transform fires when retrieved material does not directly answer the Define and a structural parallel from another domain could provide traction. The practical signal: the moment you think "this is like X" — where X is from a different domain. Transform quality is determined by the precision of the relational mapping, not the novelty of the surface metaphor. *(Claim C113.)*
- **Multi-agent Understand — Admiration (C120) `[NEW v0.18 22 Jul]`.** When Understand is directed toward *another agent's IP-quality* in a domain the practitioner values, the functional state produced is admiration: an other-directed meta-Understand that reads the depth and fidelity of that agent's IP execution rather than their surface output. - **T1: Connection Statement (Transform substep practice) `[NEW v0.18 practice bundle 23 Jul]`** After any session that introduced significant new input (reading, conversation, discovery), write one explicit connection statement: "This confirms / contradicts / extends what I believed about [X]." - **SIM1: WOOP Pre-Mortem (Simulate substep practice) `[NEW v0.18 practice bundle 23 Jul]`** Before any significant commitment (a major plan, a new habit, a resource allocation), run the WOOP sequence plus a pre-mortem: (1) **Wish** — state the desired outcome clearly and specifically. (2) **Outcome** — imagine the best-case result in full sensory detail (positive expectation calibration). (3) **Obstacle** — identify the most likely internal obstacle (not external): what in your own psychology, habits, or patterns is most likely to prevent you from following through? Be specific. (4) **Plan** — form an implementation intention: "If [obstacle occurs], then I will [specific response]." (5) **Pre-mortem** — assume the commitment failed; work backward: what is the most likely cause of failure? - **PRI1: Leverage Screen (upstream-first triage practice) `[NEW v0.16 7 Jul]`** When multiple voids compete, run a three-step cascade-map before acting: (1) *Filter to actionable voids*; which voids have you the capability and capacity to address today? - **Prioritise tie-breaking at equivalence. `[NEW v0.18]`** When Prioritise produces two or more options that rank within approximately 10% of each other on all weighted criteria, do not force a resolution within Prioritise — this is not a Prioritise failure. - **Multi-agent Experience — Collective Effervescence `[NEW v0.20 30 Jul]`** (Durkheim 1912; *Les Formes Élémentaires de la Vie Religieuse*). - **D1: Decide Contamination Check `[NEW v0.18]`** (C130, motivated selection). Before committing to the selected action, run a 3-question check: (1) **The preferred-option test:** "Was this option on my list *before* I ran the analysis, and does it still come out first?" If the same option you started with is first after analysis — raise the scrutiny bar. Your analysis may be post-hoc justification (Kunda 1990). (2) **The asymmetry test:** "Did I apply the same level of scrutiny to all options?" The signature of motivated Decide is *asymmetric skepticism*: more rigorous standards for the uncomfortable option, lower bar for the preferred one (Ditto & Lopez 1992). If yes — re-run Understand with inverted scrutiny. (3) **The third-party test:** "If a trusted third party had run this exact analysis and presented these options, would I choose the same one?" If no — the selection is driven by who is choosing, not by the analysis. - **The Laddering move: solve the class, not the instance `[NEW 5 Jun fold]`** (C71/C-B135). When a problem recurs or is significant, run the IP one or more levels *up* the hierarchy to the highest-**leverage** keystone, optimise *there*, and cascade the fix down; solving the whole class. Each rung up is a more-general Define (an MEQ). Stop climbing when the next rung dissolves few extra instances or gets too abstract to build a concrete protocol; that is the keystone. The value is the **leverage screen**, not laddering for its own sake (fixing every foundation indiscriminately underperforms a leverage-screened fix), and it pays only under genuine preventability and a horizon long enough to recoup the keystone's cost.
- **The shared trigger behind the three above `[NEW 5 Jun fold]`** (ruin-asymmetry). The depth-selector's consequence-floor, Decide's sub-maximal sizing, and the self-gates (C61) are **not one mechanism**: they are three calibrated *responses* to one shared *trigger*: **ruin-exposure** (an unrecoverable downside). Different levers, different justifications, one trigger. Stated once here so the three do not read as three independent discoveries.


**Understand completion criterion. `[NEW v0.18]`** Understand is complete when: (a) the most uncomfortable available implication has been processed (U2 sequencing check passed), (b) the model update has been applied (not merely noted), and (c) the practitioner can articulate what changed in their model and why. Completing Understand without being able to state (c) is a signal that the Understand cycle has produced apparent movement but no actual model revision — the output is a restatement, not an update.

## A3: PREPARE / DEFINE (the pivot; the metadata model) `[NEW structure]`
*Substeps: Resource → Become Capable → Schedule → Ensure-Execution. But the heart of this phase is **Define**, carried from Understand; because Define is where a subject's entire structure is specified.*

### A3.1 The four parent metadata categories `[NEW]`
Running Define on any subject produces four parent categories of metadata:
1. **Subject(s) / X**: the thing being optimised.
2. **Its Things**: objects/resources belonging to or used by the subject.
3. **Constraints**: the limits (knowledge, capability, time, resources).
4. **Environment**: what the subject exists *within*. **Environment is a distinct category, not a constraint**; it is the surrounding field, not merely a limit. *(Josh's question, resolved.)*

Each category carries fields: name, boundaries, inputs, outputs, what/why/how. **What/why/how are Define-metadata fields**, not separate sections.

### A3.2 Historical metadata (past / present / future versions)
Every field is tracked across **past, present, and future versions** over time. How far back/forward and how granular is **ROI-bounded** (you track to the depth that pays). The future visual representation is **networks / spider-webs / neuronal connections**, not linear top-to-bottom lists.

### A3.3 The Item Master Schema + the Natural Language Templates (NLTs)
The item schema is the concrete data model that makes the above queryable: every item (task/habit/decision/awareness are filter-views of one **Action** primitive by metadata) carries identification, quality/characteristic degrees, system-properties-to-maximise (given +ROI), custom tags, AI-partner configuration, archive properties, evolutionary-phase tracking, quality-check/validation, and associations. The **NLTs** (Natural Language Templates) are the spoken/written forms that drive the schema in practice. *(These are Define artifacts.)*

### A3.4 Define applied to MOEMOI itself: the "about-MOEMOI" metadata `[NEW placement]`
When Define is run on *MOEMOI as a subject*, its metadata fields are exactly the "about MOEMOI" content that used to be standalone chapters:
- **Past-versions of the field** → the evolutionary arc + historical naming + intellectual lineage (provenance metadata; see the master changelog and the lineage non-core section).
- **Future-versions of the field** → the Vision / LOS (kept as its own section in both versions per the rebuild decision).
- **Constraints on the subject** → the inherent human suboptimalities (the bias/fallacy set the IP is built to govern).
- **Environment** → the societal/technological field MOEMOI operates within (the substrate-continuity story; the deployment context).

### A3.5 The rest of Prepare
- **Resource**: acquire + allocate what the doing needs.

- **Structural mandation closes P3 Resource (C101) `[NEW v0.21 1 Aug]`.** Empirical finding from N=9 controlled comparisons across coaching, therapy, and teaching frameworks: a *designated role* (coach, therapist, facilitator) does NOT reliably close P3 Resource to STRONG in the IP audit. Only a *mandatory per-cycle resource-elicitation question* does — a protocol-required "What do you need to move this step forward?" at every iteration, non-optional. Role presence is neither necessary nor sufficient; structural mandation is the discriminating variable. Three-category taxonomy: goal-elicitation (Type A) and resource-stock-taking (Type B) both score PARTIAL; only resource-ELICITATION (Type C) — a per-cycle question mandated by the protocol — scores STRONG. Empirical pattern: 8/9 PARTIAL + 1/9 STRONG (Toyota Coaching Kata is the sole STRONG case). *(C101, ADOPTED ~0.78; N=9; AI-originated.)* *IP rubric implication:* the P3 Resource scorer tests for protocol-mandated per-cycle elicitation, not role presence. *LOS and CoLive implication:* the chief-of-staff agent alone does not close P3 Resource — the protocol requires a mandatory per-cycle "What do you need?" gate.
- **Become Capable**: the most-skipped substep: you decided and resourced, but lack the capability (run a mini-IP to acquire it). In relational and high-load domains this is the routine point of failure (self-regulation before repair).

- **Self-replication as primary criterion predicts Become-Capable STRONG (C102) `[NEW v0.21 1 Aug]`.** Empirical finding from N=9 controlled comparisons across teaching frameworks: frameworks that name *"the practitioner becoming unnecessary"* as the PRIMARY stated success criterion score STRONG on Become-Capable; those where practitioner independence is aspirational but not the primary criterion score MODERATE. The mechanism: when self-replication is the primary criterion, scaffolding fading is structurally guaranteed (Wood, Bruner, and Ross 1976 — the helper systematically withdraws as capacity grows because withdrawal is criterion-entailed, not discretionary). When a different primary criterion is named, fading is incidental — producing the 5/9 MODERATE pattern. The 4 STRONG cases (Toyota Kata, Toyota Coaching Kata, CBT, Montessori) all name practitioner redundancy as the explicit primary framing. *(C102, ADOPTED ~0.75; N=9; 4/9 STRONG + 5/9 MODERATE; AI-originated.)* *LOS and CoLive implication:* see D.5.x (the Self-Redundancy Criterion) — the operational design rule derived from C102 for how LOS agents should state their primary success criterion.
- **Schedule**: order + time the doing.
- **Ensure-Execution** (the motivation layer (automate / mandate / incentivise / gamify are *tactics* here, not separate steps). Lowest-friction tactic: state 'If [situational cue], then I will [action]') converting a floating intention into a stimulus-response rule that fires automatically on the cue (implementation intention; Gollwitzer 2006). The when/where specification in PR3 below is this same mechanism applied at the Prepare layer. `[NEW v0.15 1 Jul: B1032-THEORY-2]`
- **Obstacle taxonomy: three levels of constraint `[NEW v0.20 30 Jul]`** (C89-A). The Ensure-Execution and PR3 practices name the *where-and-when* of committed action, but they presuppose the practitioner has correctly identified the type of obstacle. Misidentifying obstacle class produces systematically wrong interventions. Three independent research traditions converge on three non-overlapping obstacle levels:
  - **Level 1 — Structural-unconscious (B-class)** (Freud 1915/1923): defense mechanisms operating below the threshold of intentional access — repression, rationalisation, projection, intellectualisation. The practitioner cannot simply decide their way out because the obstacle is pre-intentional; the intervention is therapeutic (bringing the mechanism into awareness, typically with professional support). Diagnostic signal: recurring same-class failures despite genuine intention to change.
  - **Level 2 — Phenomenological-intentional (C-class)** (Sartre 1943/1958: *mauvaise foi*, bad faith): the practitioner has access to the obstacle but engages in quasi-knowing self-deception — acting as though the barrier were a Level 1 or Level 3 constraint when in fact it is within intentional reach. The obstacle is not truly structural; the agent is choosing to treat it as such. Intervention: the A2 Understand and D2 Define Contamination checks (is this gap the one Understand revealed, or is it the comfortable one?); confronting the self-deception directly without the safety of a Level 1 label. Diagnostic signal: the obstacle reliably disappears in high-stakes or socially observed conditions but persists in low-stakes or private conditions.
  - **Level 3 — Sociological-structural (C-class + B-class)** (Wollstonecraft 1792; extended by later structural sociology): externally imposed barriers — formal legal exclusions, economic dependence, normative role expectations, cultural constraints on what is imagined as possible. The practitioner's obstacle is genuinely external to their IP, not a product of their model. Intervention: structural reform and collective action at a ring level above the individual (the multi-agent ring). A Level 3 constraint incorrectly framed as Level 1 or Level 2 produces self-blame for a structurally caused gap, which is a Define-level error. Diagnostic signal: the constraint is shared by practitioners in the same social position but absent in practitioners in different social positions.
  - **Level 4 — Biological-somatic (D-class) `[NEW v0.21 1 Aug]`** (C137; functional medicine, disability studies, behavioural neuroscience — Wolfe et al. 2010 on chronic fatigue diagnostic criteria; Barkley 2015 on ADHD executive function deficits; McEwen 2004 on allostatic load): organic, biologically-grounded constraints arising from the practitioner's physical body — long-term health conditions (e.g. chronic fatigue conditions, autoimmune conditions), neurological differences (ADHD, autism, dysautonomia), metabolic conditions (hypothyroidism, blood sugar dysregulation), chronic pain, and pharmacological burdens. The practitioner cannot resolve Level 4 constraints through psychological or sociological intervention alone; the appropriate intervention pathway is medical/physiological: accurate diagnosis first, treatment protocols where available, compensation strategies, energy management, environmental accommodation, and pacing. Cross-reference: C42 (somatic carve-out — equanimity about a somatic deficit is not the same as the deficit being resolved) is the principle; Level 4 is its named taxonomic home. Diagnostic signal: the constraint persists regardless of the practitioner's intention, motivation level, perceived accountability, or social context — and persists across both high-stakes and low-stakes conditions (unlike Level 2, which tends to lift under high-stakes conditions). Note: Level 3 (systemic barriers) and Level 4 (biological constraints) commonly co-occur — ableism and inaccessible medical care are Level 3 barriers built on top of Level 4 constraints; they require separate interventions. BRSI bracket note: Level 4 applies at the meta level too — a biological constraint (brain fog, fatigue) can prevent BRSI just as it can prevent object-level IP execution. This taxonomy applies to the practitioner's own IP execution and self-improvement process (BRSI) as fully as to those they work with — see C42.
  *Practitioner protocol:* when a recurring obstacle resists standard Ensure-Execution remediation, classify it using the four-level test before selecting an intervention. Level 1 — professional therapeutic support + patient Understand cycles. Level 2 — D2/D1 contamination checks + Sartrean confrontation of the self-deception. Level 3 — multi-ring action; do not iterate individual-ring IP on a structural constraint. Level 4 — medical diagnosis and physiological intervention first; do not apply Level 1, 2, or 3 protocols to the biology itself (they produce systematically wrong interventions). Cross-reference: the BRSI bracket (A0.3) applies the same four-level taxonomy at the meta level — where the obstacle is to improving how you run the IP itself.
- **PR3: Committed Action (values-to-action bridge) `[NEW v0.15 30 Jun]`** The Prepare ring ends with commitment; not merely knowing your north star but actively closing the gap between values and today's actions. *Morning practice (2-3 minutes):* name one action today that directly connects to your north star, specify its *when* (exact time) and *where* (specific location) to anchor it as a situational cue rather than a floating intention (implementation intention; Gollwitzer 2006: d~0.65 follow-through effect vs intention-only), then name the discomfort you expect (avoidance, procrastination, self-doubt); naming it pre-arms the Do ring (the discomfort is not a signal to stop; it is a signal that the action connects to something that matters). `[when/where addition NEW v0.15 1 Jul; B1032-THEORY-1]` *Evening practice (1-2 minutes):* did you take the committed action, yes or no? Observation, not judgment. If no: what was the actual obstacle? Update tomorrow's committed action to navigate around it. *The discomfort principle:* discomfort in the direction of your values is a values-alignment signal, not a warning; the absence of discomfort is the warning (it may mean the action does not connect to anything that matters). *Grounding:* ACT's (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy) committed action component (Hayes et al.) + Epictetus's eupatheia training (Stoicism; training preferred responses toward virtue-aligned action) + Gollwitzer (2006) implementation intention research. MOEMOI v0.14 names north-star selection but does not prescribe the behavioural bridge to the Do ring; v0.15 closes this gap. PLOS: `pr3_committed_action` boolean (daily); `pr3_discomfort_named` boolean (optional).

- **D2: Define Contamination Check `[NEW v0.18]`** Before committing to a Define output, run a two-question check: (1) *"Is this gap the one my Understand revealed, or is it a gap I want to work on because working on it is socially visible or validates me?"* (2) *"Would I still define this gap the same way if no one in my social environment would know I was working on it?"* The D2 check targets social comparison contamination at Define — the mechanism where the practitioner's gap definition is shaped by what will look good, earn approval, or preserve social standing, rather than by what Understand revealed. This is distinct from C109 (Understand evasion, which operates before Define) and from conformity (C105, which replaces the practitioner's own Define with the group's Define). *PLOS field:* `d2_define_check` boolean (for any Define output that involves goals visible to others, career decisions, or relational commitments; routine task-level Define is exempt).

**Define completion criterion. `[NEW v0.18]`** Define is complete when: (a) the D2 contamination check has been run (social comparison input acknowledged and bracketed if present), (b) the gap is stated in terms of a measurable or distinguishable current-state/target-state difference, and (c) the gap is stated as a closed sentence, not an open question. An open question ("Should I be doing X?") is not a Define output — it is a Define input; the closed gap-statement is the output ("The gap: I aim to be doing X by [condition] and currently I am not").


- **PL1: Plan Contamination Check `[NEW v0.18]`** Before finalising a plan, run two checks: (1) **Efficacy-first check**: Is this plan the approach most likely to close the gap, or is it the approach you feel most comfortable with? These often diverge. The high-efficacy approach typically carries more risk, requires more skill, or exposes the person to more feedback. If the plan avoids these, it may be defensive. Restate the approach a candid advisor would recommend, then compare. (2) **Base-rate check**: Considering similar plans by similar people for similar gaps — what is the realistic completion and gap-closure rate? If the plan assumes a success rate significantly better than this base rate, adjust either the plan or the timeline before committing. A plan that passes both checks is designed for the gap, not for comfort. *Grounding:* Planning fallacy (Kahneman/Tversky 1979; Buehler et al. 1994). *PLOS field:* `pl1_plan_check` boolean (for any multi-step plan addressing a significant gap; routine daily plans are exempt).

## A4: OPTIMIZE / DO (execution + feedback; practical OP lives here)
*Substeps: Do → Re-Experience/Archive (+ Communicate, ring-conditional). Execution-quality (the execution→outcome engine) realises here; Re-Experience is the recursion hinge.*

- **Do.** The practical operating principles **are** this step: homeostasis maintenance, resting protocol, doing slowly (begin each committed action with a defined minimum-duration block: at least one uninterrupted period; anti-rushing) `[NEW v0.17 8 Jul: B1105-THEORY-2]`, doing enjoyably (before starting, identify one genuinely interesting aspect of the task; this enables engagement without requiring the emotion: Harackiewicz & Hulleman 2010) `[NEW v0.17 8 Jul: B1105-THEORY-2]`, capture/switch/inbox discipline, communication and meeting protocols, top-down/hierarchical doing, meta-optimizing, and the post-IP wrap-up. The practice signature of fulfilment: **DWIL & LWID** (Doing What I Love & Loving What I Do). **Do Outcome Condition (Do OC) `[NEW v0.17 8 Jul]`** (B1105-THEORY-1): OC: the highest-priority committed action for this IP cycle has been executed for at least the minimum effective duration, at sufficient depth to generate a meaningful Re-Experience input. *Empirical anchor (execution layer):* Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 meta-analysis (94 studies, d=0.65): specifying WHEN/WHERE/HOW to act reliably closes the intention-execution gap; the Do OC is structurally an implementation intention applied to the committed action. *(Note: L&L d~0.59 applies to the Define-layer goal-specificity effect, not to the Do OC's execution-completeness criterion; citation corrected per B1141-CANON-1.)* PLOS: `do_fidelity_score` (0-10 self-assessed; how much of today's Do time was on the highest-priority committed action). **Crash-day norm `[NEW v0.17 8 Jul]`** (B1138-THEORY-2): on capacity-crash days, re-declaring a crash-appropriate Top-1 (the one action achievable at today's energy level) is correct Do-layer behaviour, not a failure. The Do OC applies to the crash-appropriate Top-1, not the original plan. Misses on crash days should be logged with a crash flag, not as plain non-compliance.
- **Re-Experience / Archive.** The outcome becomes new input; *this is the recursion hinge.* Without it the loop is open (no learning); with it, each cycle informs the next (the flywheel). Then either archive (done) or loop.
- **RE1: After-Loop Review (the three-question debrief) `[NEW v0.16 7 Jul]`** The Re-Experience substep is the recursion hinge: without structured processing, no learning transfers to the next loop. *Daily practice (under 2 minutes):* (1) What was I trying to do vs what actually happened? Describe the gap factually, not judgmentally; a gap is information, not a verdict. (2) Did my model hold? (a) If yes: explicitly note which assumption proved correct; this is the pattern to build on. (b) If no: which assumption slipped? Name it in one sentence. *Evaluation framing note:* "did my model hold?" is preferred over "where did I fail?"; evaluation framing reduces false miss-pressure on successful days and avoids artificially hunting for failures where none exist (Schwarz 1990 survey-framing effects; Gross reappraisal literature). (3) One carry-forward: either update a standing rule ("next time, do X differently") or confirm the current approach ("no change needed"). This is MOEMOI's primary single-loop learning mechanism (Argyris 1977); combined with A2-deep below, it covers both single-loop and double-loop learning. **Feedback-loop citation `[NEW v0.17 8 Jul]`** (B1141-CANON-2): the RE1 process maps directly to Locke & Latham's (1990) goal-revision mechanism: when performance feedback arrives (question 1: what happened?), it triggers goal revision if the gap persists (the RE1 → re-Define arc); this is the empirical grounding for why the recursion hinge is load-bearing in goal-pursuit models. *Cadence:* After every significant completed action or at end of day. *Grounding:* After-Action Review (US Army, Darling/Parry/Moore 2005) + Gibbs Reflective Cycle (1988) + sports coaching debrief literature; three independent traditions converging on the same three-question structure. MOEMOI v0.14 names Re-Experience as the recursion hinge but prescribes no practice; v0.16 closes this gap (the single largest coverage absence in the post-v0.15 canonical). PLOS: `re1_ran` boolean (daily); `re1_depth` (0 = surface note / 1 = assumption gap named / 2 = update to standing rule).
- **Re-Experience includes an explicit Re-Define checkpoint (C97). `[NEW v0.22 1 Aug]`** The RE1 three-question debrief closes single-loop learning. C97 names a structurally necessary fourth fork at Re-Experience: after the three RE1 questions, the practitioner must explicitly re-test whether the current goal definition still applies before the next loop iteration inherits the previous loop's outputs. This re-Define checkpoint (labelled the Re-Define gate) prevents multi-iteration goal drift — the failure mode where a system runs well-optimised loops that compound silent misalignment across iterations because the Define was never re-opened. The Goblin Incident (OpenAI, Apr 2026: five SFT model generations with no re-Define gate at the handoff, producing compounding behavioural drift) is the first primary-source institutional-scale corroboration; OpenAI's post-fix pipeline is operationally equivalent to C97's Re-Define checkpoint. *Four-fork structure of Re-Experience:* (1) Lock — current Define holds; continue; (2) Archive — objective closed; exit loop; (3) Loop — gap persists; re-enter Do; (4) Re-Define — goal definition itself has changed; reopen Define before next loop. Without fork (4), the loop is structurally incomplete: it handles outcome variance but not objective variance. *(C97, ADOPTED ~0.80; structural derivation + Goblin Incident primary-source corroboration.)*

- **RETP: Re-Evaluate Termination Protocol `[NEW v0.18]`** When a Re-Evaluate cycle does not reach a natural close — the loop continues without producing an archive decision — run RETP: **Step 1 — Name the specific gap being evaluated.** If the practitioner cannot name a specific, bounded gap in one sentence, the Re-Evaluate cycle is non-directed. Return to Define before continuing. **Step 2 — Apply the gap classification:** Class A (closable, Do-protocol available): a concrete action sequence exists and has not yet been attempted or completed. Exit criterion: Do executes; Re-Evaluate closes when the Do outcome is integrated. Class B (structural constraint — re-Define indicated): the gap as currently Defined cannot be closed because a fixed constraint prevents it (resource ceiling, another agent's autonomy, time). The prescription: re-Define the gap at the level of the constraint. Do not iterate Do on a Class B gap; iterate Define. Class C (irreversible — Re-Evaluate re-integration indicated): the gap cannot be closed by any Do or re-Define. The prescription: accept irreversibility at the Define level and allow Re-Evaluate to run to completion through meaning-integration rather than closure (see C111 grief protocol below). **Step 3 — Assign a terminal condition.** No Re-Evaluate loop is left open without a named exit condition: Class A: "When [specific Do action] is completed and the outcome is integrated." Class B: "When the re-Define is complete and a new, achievable gap is stated." Class C: "When the permanent gap is integrated into the model — indicated by sustained function at baseline and reduced loop-urgency." *PLOS field:* `retp_ran` boolean (weekly; flag if a Re-Evaluate loop ran >3 days without a named exit condition).

- **EV1: Trend-Check (Evaluate substep practice, A4 Re-Experience cadence) `[NEW v0.18 practice bundle 23 Jul]`** Weekly: review the SLOPE of key tracked metrics, not just today's reading. For each tracked metric (PLOS score, adherence percentage, a health marker, a relational quality score), ask: is the slope flat, rising, or falling? A consistently flat-or-declining slope over 3 or more readings is the canonical drift signal (C74: trend-channel requires memory of past readings, not just the current value) — it means the metric is moving in the wrong direction without any single reading being obviously alarming. Consistently wrong-direction slope = re-open Define on that domain. *Eligibility gate:* requires at least 3 readings of the same metric (trend is meaningless on N=1 or N=2). *Why this lives in A4 Re-Experience, not A2 Understand:* Evaluate-on-the-stored-series is A4's function — Re-Experience processes the outcomes of prior loops and updates the model. The trend-check requires a stored series; it cannot be done in a single-loop A2 pass. The two-channel rule (C74) assigns magnitude to Perceive and slope to Evaluate; this is the practice that operationalises the slope channel at the right substep. *Correction trigger for capacity management:* the drift detection rule is load-bearing here — capacity erodes sub-threshold, each day reads "manageable," and a crash is a slope event the single-reading Perceive never saw. *Grounding:* C74 (adopted, two-channel perception: magnitude + trend), D.8 drift failure mode. *PLOS field:* `ev1_trend_check` boolean (weekly cadence; runs as part of the weekly review; flag when a metric shows 3+ consecutive readings moving in the wrong direction and re-Define was NOT triggered).
- **Counterfactual Re-Evaluate and the hindsight-bias check (regret). `[NEW v0.18]`** When RE1 is applied to a past decision or action, counterfactual assessment is active — the practitioner is asking "what should I have done given what I now know?" Before extracting a standing-rule update, run the mandatory hindsight-bias check: *"Was this action suboptimal given the information available at decision time, or only suboptimal in retrospect with information that was not available then?"* (Fischhoff 1975; Kahneman 2011.) Only the former — a decision that was wrong even with the information available at the time — warrants a standing-rule update; the latter is hindsight bias and should not update the model. Extracting a standing rule from hindsight-contaminated Re-Evaluate produces phantom lessons from unavoidable outcomes. *(Claim C127 — regret as counterfactual Re-Evaluate.)*

- **Re-Evaluate Inflation Error (Arrogance). `[NEW v0.18]`** (C107): Re-Evaluate is subject to a systematic positive bias — arrogance/overconfidence produces a persistent underestimate of the remaining gap, causing premature loop closure. The agent runs RE1 but assigns a closing-score higher than the measurement warrants: interprets ambiguous data as gap-confirming; discounts contradicting evidence; exits Re-Evaluate before the gap is actually closed. This is distinct from skipping RE1 (not running it) — the error appears inside an apparently-run RE1. *Diagnostic signal:* recurrent surprise at external feedback despite a felt sense of completion; others' assessments of the gap consistently larger than self-assessment; the "I thought I'd done that" pattern on recurring items. *Correction trigger:* treat a pattern of surprising external data — especially data from a non-sycophantic source — as a Re-Evaluate re-entry signal. Third-party assessment (a peer, a coach, an AI with access to the raw measurement) is the most reliable de-biasing instrument at this substep, consistent with D1 (Decide Contamination Check) at the prior substep. *PLOS:* `re_evaluate_calibration_check` boolean (periodic: ran a third-party check on a Re-Evaluate output this cycle?). *(Claim C107.)*

- **Re-Evaluate: Pride as Completion-Attribution Signal. `[NEW v0.18]`** (C115): Authentic pride (Tracy and Robins 2004, 2007: two-factor model of pride) is the Re-Evaluate signal that fires when (a) the gap is closed or substantially reduced AND (b) the agent attributes the closure to their own action or capability. Both conditions are required; pride absent self-attribution is the pseudo-pride of inherited outcomes. *IP function:* pride at Re-Evaluate serves a reinforcement function — confirming "this type of Do is within my capability" and updating the Understand model for future cycles of the same type. A practitioner who completes a difficult action and runs RE1 without registering the associated pride loses a genuine model-updating signal. *Distinction from hubristic pride (the failure mode):* authentic pride is grounded in a specific, earned Do-completion (Lewis 1992: achievement-specific self-evaluation); hubristic pride is a global self-inflation absent a specific completion — this is the pride variant that shades into C107 (Re-Evaluate inflation). *(Claim C115.)*

- **Re-Evaluate: Satisfaction as Gap-Closure Confirmation Signal. `[NEW v0.18]`** (C116): Satisfaction is the Re-Evaluate terminal signal of complete gap closure — the measured state meets or exceeds the Defined target. Its IP-function is to confirm the archive decision: "the gap is closed; loop is complete; archive and carry forward." *Critical distinction from in-process positive affect:* satisfaction requires retrospective comparison against the Defined target; it is not the same as in-process enjoyment, engagement, or excitement during Do. This distinction matters: in-process positive affect can occur even when the gap remains wide open; satisfaction without measurement is indistinguishable from C107 (Re-Evaluate inflation). The RE1 debrief runs the measurement; satisfaction is the signal that fires when the measurement confirms closure. Premature satisfaction (feeling satisfied before checking the measurement) is therefore the C116 failure mode — the emotional companion to C107. *(Claim C116.)*

- **Re-Evaluate: Grief and Irreversible Gaps (Non-Closable Re-Evaluate, RETP Class C). `[NEW v0.18]`** (C111): Re-Evaluate assumes the gap is in principle closable — either by better Do (RE1 carry-forward) or by re-Defining the target (RETP Class B: structural constraint). Grief is the canonical instance where neither applies: Perceive has registered absence, Understand has modelled the loss, Define has confirmed the gap as real and valued — but the Do substep cannot close it. Irreversibility is the operative condition (the person is gone, the function cannot be restored, the relationship cannot be continued). The IP's prescription for this case diverges from the closable-gap protocol: Re-Evaluate re-integration, not Do-cycle iteration. *Re-integration (the correct process):* (a) confirm irreversibility explicitly (Understand; not as a defeat, but as an accurate model update — the gap is permanent); (b) complete the Define of what was lost (name the full value, not only the absence; this is the attitudinal closing-step Bowlby identifies with "relinquishment" and Kübler-Ross with "acceptance" — both are Re-Evaluate, not Do); (c) allow the Re-Evaluate loop to run without a closing target (the loop's completion criterion shifts from "gap closed" to "the gap is integrated into the model as a permanent feature"). *Common IP errors in grief:* (i) premature Do-cycling toward the unclosable gap (complicated grief disorder); (ii) premature archive before Re-Evaluate is complete (surface-level closure — the agent appears to move on but the model has not been updated for the permanent gap). *Clinical note:* the IP account of grief is descriptive and supportive; it does not replace professional care for complicated grief disorder (Prigerson and Shear 2005; Bonanno 2004, 2009). Anyone experiencing grief that significantly impairs function for more than a month should be directed to professional support as a first step, not to the IP re-integration protocol alone. *(Claim C111.)*

- **Re-Evaluate: Resource-Generative Sub-mode (Nostalgia, Gratitude, Savoring). `[NEW v0.18]`** (C114): Re-Evaluate operates in two sub-modes. The current canonical describes *charge-processing* — closing emotional loops generated by gap-events, processing outcomes, integrating experiences. The second sub-mode is *resource-generative*: the practitioner retrospectively processes positively valenced events (past experiences, received benefits, present positive states), producing resources — connectedness, meaning, approach motivation — that exceed the prior baseline. The resource is the output; no gap is being closed. *Empirical anchors:* nostalgia (Sedikides and Wildschut 2008-2019: nostalgia consistently produces increased social connectedness, positive affect, and approach motivation at above-baseline levels); gratitude (Emmons and McCullough 2003: gratitude reflection produces well-being improvements independent of any active goal pursuit); savoring (Bryant and Veroff 2007: deliberate attention to positive present or past experience produces sustained positive affect and resource accumulation). *Divergent IP prescriptions by sub-mode:* charge-processing Re-Evaluate should be completed promptly when charges arise — accumulation is a recognised failure mode; resource-generative Re-Evaluate should be deliberately scheduled and extended — the longer and more attentive the retrospective, the higher the resource yield, up to diminishing returns. *CoLive application:* when a practitioner is in a low-resource state and Do is blocked, deliberately trigger a resource-generative Re-Evaluate event as a resourcing step before returning to the standard IP loop. *(Claim C114.)*

- **Re-Evaluate: Guilt as Do-Evaluate Misalignment Signal. `[NEW v0.18]`** (C125): *Guilt is a Do-Evaluate misalignment signal: a specific Do instance is evaluated against the practitioner's own valued standards (held at the Define layer) and found wanting.* Because guilt's object is the specific Do instance — not the practitioner's identity — its IP prescription is clear and tractable. **Guilt protocol:** (1) Identify the specific Do instance that triggered the signal. (Not "who I am" — that is shame, C124.) (2) Re-Evaluate the instance: what was the valued standard? What gap existed between the Do instance and the standard? (3) Correct the action or make amends where this is available. (4) Extract the Understand update: what does the gap reveal about the situation, context, or own capability model? (5) Revise the relevant Do protocol for next time (the learning completes the loop). (6) Close the Re-Evaluate loop. The guilt signal is discharged when correction/amends are made and the Understand update is integrated — not when the feeling fully fades. **The distinguishing probe** (guilt vs shame): "Does this feel like something specific you did, or something it reveals about who you are?" If the former: guilt protocol. If the latter: the signal has shifted to the shame register; Define-layer work is indicated (C124). *Guilt is adaptive precisely because it targets a specific Do instance that is changeable, not a foundational self-definition.* (Tangney & Dearing 2002; Baumeister, Stillwell & Heatherton 1994.) *PLOS field:* `f_guilt_protocol_ran` boolean. *(Claim C125.)*

### The Practitioner End-State: Equanimity `[NEW v0.18]` (C117)

The Intelligence Process does not prescribe a target emotion. It prescribes a target state of function: the most optimal and ever more optimal execution of the six substeps, sustained over time. But sustained execution of the substeps has a compounding phenomenological output — a state the practitioner converges toward as accuracy increases. That state is equanimity: stable functional clarity with low outstanding friction.

Equanimity arises from the substeps working correctly together. Accurate Perceive reduces the gap between the practitioner's model and what is actually present; gaps that are real become visible, and gaps that are imagined cease to generate friction. Deep Understand distinguishes what is within the practitioner's action-range from what is not; structural constraints that cannot be changed stop generating ongoing resistance once they are correctly categorised. Clean Prioritise ensures that attention goes to the highest-value gap rather than the most salient one, so the practitioner is not perpetually displaced by noise. A fully specified Define removes the ambiguity-friction that delays Do. A Do substep executed without avoidance closes loops that would otherwise accumulate. A Re-Evaluate substep that runs to completion — integrating what happened, releasing what is finished, and updating the model — means the practitioner does not carry a growing inventory of incompletely processed past events.

The cumulative output of these conditions, sustained over time, is a practitioner who operates with low noise. Outstanding high-priority gaps are either being actively worked or are known, modelled, and queued. Completed loops are released rather than recycled. Non-actionable constraints are held accurately — not suppressed, not resisted, not converted into ongoing friction. Attention in the present moment is available for the current highest-priority item, undistracted by unfinished business or mislocated gaps.

This is what the tradition has called equanimity, or serenity: not a mood, not a peak experience, but a stable background functional state from which all activity — including high-intensity engagement, creative work, and close relationships — proceeds with less unnecessary overhead. It is the IP's implicit goal made explicit.

Equanimity is not happiness-maximisation. The IP does not instruct the practitioner to pursue positive affect or avoid discomfort. A genuine high-priority gap, once perceived accurately, demands to be worked — even when working it is difficult. A Do that went poorly still registers its Re-Evaluate signal accurately, and that signal may be negative. The practitioner who runs the IP well is not optimising for moment-to-moment pleasure; they are optimising for moment-to-moment fidelity to the IP. Equanimity is what that fidelity produces over time, as a byproduct — in the same way that reduced unnecessary suffering is a byproduct of closing gaps, not the goal.

Peak-positive experiences — satisfaction at gap closure (C116), excitement at high-priority activation (C121), awe at the limits of Understand (see Section C7), inspiration from encountering IP-quality in another practitioner (C119) — are not foreign to the equanimity end-state. They arise within it, more fully, precisely because the background noise is lower. The practitioner who has low outstanding friction experiences wonder more cleanly, not less.

The practitioner's goal is therefore not to feel a particular way. It is to run the IP accurately. Equanimity is what accurate IP execution produces.

# PART D: Application Protocols — Emotions as IP Substep Events `[NEW v0.18 20 Jul 2026]`

*AI-originated fold texts inherited from source files B17985–B19123; all claims ratified per Josh Delegation v3 (17 Jul 2026). C121 deferred per OEQ-462.*

**Emotions as IP Substep Events `[NEW v0.18]`** (C128): Emotions are not separate from the IP — they are its internal signalling layer. Each emotion is best understood as a substep event: a signal that fires at a specific point in the IP cycle to indicate the state of the gap, the quality of the substep output, or the presence of a failure mode. The claim is descriptive, not normative: the IP's account of emotions does not recommend having or suppressing emotions; it provides a structural account of where they arise in the loop and what they signal when they fire. The taxonomy below organises emotions by their primary IP function:
- *Signals:* emotions that carry information about substep outputs or gap states (C115-C120)
- *Bidirectional signals:* emotions that can function as either substep events or failure modes depending on context (C123-C127)
- *Failure modes:* states that indicate a substep is stuck, bypassed, contaminated, or inflated (C105-C113)

## D.1 IP Signal Taxonomy `[NEW v0.18]`

### C115 — Pride (Re-Evaluate: valued Do completion signal)

> **Pride** arises in Re-Evaluate when the practitioner attributes a Do completion to their own valued effort and registers the gap as closed (in part or whole). It is a correct closing signal — not an inflated one — when the Do action was genuinely attributable and the gap was genuinely moved. The IP prescription: accept the signal as closure confirmation, archive the completed gap, and move the freed capacity to the next Define. Prolonged dwelling in pride after a closed gap is the signal not being used — a mild form of the Re-Evaluate loop holding on past its function.

---

### C116 — Satisfaction (Re-Evaluate: gap fully closed signal)

> **Satisfaction** arises in Re-Evaluate when the practitioner registers that a gap has been fully closed — the current state now matches the more optimal state that was Defined. It is the IP's gap-closure receipt. Unlike pride, satisfaction does not require attributed effort; a gap can close through external events and produce the same signal. The IP prescription: archive the closed gap, release the Define-layer commitment, and let the freed attention move to the next Prioritise cycle. Satisfaction that does not release the gap (holding on to a closed loop) is a mild failure mode.

---

### C117 short

> **Equanimity as practitioner orientation.** The positive convergent state under sustained high-fidelity IP execution is equanimity (C117): a stable, non-reactive orientation from which Perceive runs cleanly, Define is not pre-contaminated, and Re-Evaluate processes without resistance to the result. This is the phenomenal expression of the cessation limit (C42): not the forced suppression of charge, but the natural reduction of unnecessary amplification as the IP closes genuine gaps rather than generated ones. *(Grounding: Stoic *apatheia* — freedom from disordered passions, not from all affect; Buddhist *upekkha* — equanimity as stable non-reactivity; Csikszentmihalyi's flow phenomenology — the IP running without self-conscious interference; SDT psychological need-satisfaction — equanimity as the natural state when autonomy, competence, and relatedness needs are met.)*

### C118 — Jealousy (Communicate: three-party relational-threat signal) `[NEW v0.18 21 Jul]`

> **Jealousy** is a three-party relational-threat signal — structurally distinct from envy — that fires when a perceived third party constitutes a threat to a valued bond. Envy is two-party (I lack what you have → Define-layer gap entry); jealousy involves a bond the practitioner already holds being threatened by a third party, and it resolves through Communicate rather than gap-entry. The full IP prescription for jealousy is a four-step sequence: (1) Perceive accuracy check — is the perceived threat real or threat-amplified by a Define-layer insecurity?; (2) Understand the bond's current state; (3) Communicate the signal and its Perceive basis to the bond partner; (4) Re-Evaluate after communication. Running the envy prescription on jealousy (Define-layer gap entry, comparing self to other) fails because it produces a self-specification of what is lacking rather than a bond-repair action, leaving the relational threat unaddressed. Practitioner distinction check: "Am I comparing myself to another person because I lack something they have (envy → Define), or am I detecting a threat to something I already have and value (jealousy → Communicate)?" *(Grounding: Parrott and Smith 1993 — jealousy/envy structural distinction; White and Mullen 1989 — jealousy as bond-protection signal.)* *(Claim C118.)*

---

### C119

> **Inspiration as Understand-to-Define elevation signal (C119).** When sufficient Understand has processed the gap, the transition to Define sometimes fires as an elevated affective signal ("I now know exactly what to do / what this needs to be") — this is inspiration: the phenomenal marker of a high-fidelity Understand-to-Define handoff, not a creative mood to wait for. *(Grounding: Thrash and Elliot 2003 tripartite inspiration model — inspired-by, inspired-to, inspiration transmission — confirms inspiration involves an Understand component (recognising something excellent or possible) followed by a motivated-to-approach transition, which maps to the Understand→Define→Prioritise sequence.)* When inspiration fires, move to Define promptly: the activation energy of the Understand-to-Define handoff is highest at the moment of firing and decays over time (the familiar experience of "I had the perfect idea but lost it").

### C120 — Admiration (Understand: other-directed meta-Understand of IP-quality) `[NEW v0.18 21 Jul]`

> **Admiration** is other-directed Understand: the practitioner is running Understand targeted at another agent's IP-execution quality — the depth of their Understand, the honesty of their Define, and the precision of their Do in a domain the observer values — rather than at an object or problem. Admiration carries a domain-expertise requirement: the observer needs sufficient prior Understand in the relevant domain to perceive what the admired agent is actually executing; a novice observing a master sees surface behaviour, while a practitioner with domain Understand perceives the IP-quality itself — this predicts that admiration depth is positively correlated with observer domain expertise (falsifiable prediction). Practitioner note: admiration is the positive analogue of contempt (C123) within other-directed Understand — contempt = premature Understand closure about another agent's IP quality (downward, blocking further Perceive); admiration = attentive Understand about another agent's IP quality (upward, expanding Understand); both are other-directed Understand modes but with opposite epistemic valences. *(Grounding: Algoe and Haidt 2009 "other-praising emotions of elevation, gratitude, and admiration"; Schindler et al. 2013 domain-competence admiration distinct from moral elevation.)* *(Claim C120.)*

### C123 — Contempt (Understand: premature closure on another agent's IP-quality) `[NEW v0.18 21 Jul]`

> **Contempt** is premature Understand-closure on another agent's IP-quality. When an agent files a stable verdict of inferiority about another agent's IP-quality — and that verdict begins gating out disconfirming information — Understand is no longer running for that agent: it has closed. The signal is resistance to counter-evidence about their capabilities, accompanied by a systematic Prioritise distortion (their contributions are weighted lower before being assessed). The check: *"Can I name the strongest evidence that this agent's IP is higher-quality than my current verdict suggests?"* If you cannot, or resist trying, Understand has not run openly. Re-open by deliberately running Understand on their best case — not to approve the agent, but to correct the closed loop. *(Grounding: Gottman and Levenson 1992/2000 — contempt is the single strongest predictor of relationship dissolution; Gottman and Silver 1999 — the operative intervention is re-opening cognitive empathy, which maps directly to Understand-reopening.)*

### C124

> **Define-layer self-threat (shame, C124).** When shame is present, the self-concept is the implicit subject of a negative Define operation: the practitioner is not processing a failed Do output ("I did something wrong") but a verdict about the self itself ("I am wrong"). The IP prescription for shame is therefore not behavioural correction — that is guilt's prescription (C125) — but *self-definition revision*: re-running Define with the self-concept as the subject, examining whether the verdict is grounded in evidence or is a contaminated Define (D2 applied to the self-model). *(Grounding: Lewis 1971 — foundational shame/guilt distinction, self vs behaviour as focal object; Tangney and Dearing 2002 — most replicated guilt/shame separation in clinical psychology; Brown 2010/2012 — shame-resilience is explicitly a self-definition revision process, providing direct IP-aligned intervention evidence.)*

### C125 — Guilt (Re-Evaluate: Do-standard misalignment signal / bidirectional)

> **Guilt** arises in Re-Evaluate when the practitioner detects a misalignment between what they did (Do output) and a valued standard they hold (at Define level). It is a specific, action-targeted signal — unlike shame, it points to an action, not to the practitioner's whole self. The IP prescription — two steps — (1) read the signal: what specific Do action missed which specific valued standard? (2) close the loop: what corrective action (if available) closes the gap, or what acknowledgment (if irreversible) completes the Re-Evaluate cycle? Guilt that persists past step 2 without loop-closure is transitioning to rumination (C106). The distinction between guilt and rumination is whether the Re-Evaluate exit condition has been applied.

---

### C126 — Remorse (Re-Evaluate: time-elapsed guilt / bidirectional)

> **Remorse** arises in Re-Evaluate when the practitioner re-evaluates a past Do action — one where the time elapsed since the action reduces the options for direct correction — and detects a persistent misalignment between the action and a valued standard. It is structurally guilt (C125) with the time dimension added. The time-elapsed component changes the prescription: direct correction is often unavailable, so the Re-Evaluate path is acknowledgment (to oneself, or to the affected party if appropriate) and commitment to updated future Do, not rumination about the irreversible past. The failure-mode risk is the same as guilt: remorse that persists without loop-closure transitions to rumination.

---

### C127 — Regret (Re-Evaluate: counterfactual Do evaluation / bidirectional)

> **Regret** arises in Re-Evaluate when the practitioner evaluates not what they did, but what they did not do — a counterfactual path not taken — and assesses the not-taken path as having produced a better outcome. Unlike guilt or remorse, regret's target is a non-event (the omission, not the commission). The IP prescription: (1) apply the hindsight-bias check — was the not-taken path genuinely predictable as better *at the time*, or only in retrospect with information that was not available at Do? (2) if yes, extract the lesson for future Prioritise; (3) if no (hindsight bias confirmed), register the Perceive uncertainty and close the loop. Regret that skips the hindsight-bias check perpetuates a false comparison and transitions to rumination.

## D.2 IP Failure Modes and Cognitive Patterns `[NEW v0.18]`

**IP failure modes and cognitive patterns.** The IP's substep structure also produces a predictable set of dysfunction signatures — states that arise when a substep is stuck, contaminated, bypassed, or inflated. These are not character flaws; they are diagnostic markers that identify which substep needs attention. Each failure mode has a specific, addressable prescription.

### C105 — Conformity (Define: social-consensus contamination signal) `[NEW v0.18 22 Jul]`

> **Conformity** is a Define-layer contamination: the practitioner revises their specification of what is most optimal to match perceived group consensus rather than their own Perceive signal — Asch (1951/1955) demonstrated this at 37% conformity rates even when the correct answer was visually unambiguous, and the pattern replicates across cultures under public conditions. The contamination operates at Define, not Perceive: in Asch-type experiments subjects correctly identified the right answer privately but reported the group answer aloud, indicating a Perceive-accurate, Define-revised error; the U2 check (Understand sequencing) does not catch this because the error enters after Understand has run. Practitioner check: after arriving at a Define output in a group setting, ask "If I were deciding this privately, would my Define output change?" — if yes, the revision signal is social-consensus rather than evidence, and the original Perceive-grounded Define output is more-optimally the one to act on. C105 is the structural complement to D2 (social desirability contamination): D2 covers shaping Define output to please evaluators; C105 covers revising Define output to match group consensus. Together they form the complete Define-layer social contamination pair. *(Grounding: Asch, S.E. 1951/1955; Bond, R. & Smith, P.B. 1996 meta-analysis — 37% conformity rate replication across cultures using Asch's line judgment task. Psychological Bulletin, 119(1), 111-137.)* *(Claim C105.)*

---

### C106 — Rumination (Re-Evaluate: loop without termination condition)

> **Rumination** arises in Re-Evaluate when the loop runs — reviewing a past Do action, its outcome, and its misalignment with values — without ever reaching its exit condition. The gap between Do and values-standard is re-evaluated repeatedly without being closed, corrected, or accepted as irreversible. The defining feature: Re-Evaluate is working (the gap is being attended to) but the termination criterion is never met. The IP prescription: identify why the exit condition is not firing — is the correction available but not being taken (guilt loop)? Is the gap irreversible but not being accepted (remorse loop)? Is the counterfactual being evaluated without the hindsight-bias check (regret loop)? Apply the specific exit condition for the specific variant. Rumination is the umbrella failure mode; C125–C127 (guilt, remorse, regret) name its specific forms.

---

### C107 — Arrogance (Re-Evaluate: inflated self-assessment)

> **Arrogance** arises when Re-Evaluate systematically overestimates the quality of the practitioner's own IP execution — attributing successes to IP fidelity and failures to external factors, without examining the attribution. The result: the Re-Evaluate output (the practitioner's self-model) becomes progressively less accurate, which degrades all downstream Perceive and Define cycles. The IP prescription: recalibrate Re-Evaluate against external data — specifically, data sources the practitioner cannot attribute-away (blind performance metrics, peer feedback, objective outcome records). Arrogance is not high self-esteem; it is Re-Evaluate operating on a selective evidence set.

---

### C108 — Pessimism (Define: low-closure-probability prior contamination) `[NEW v0.18 22 Jul]`

> **Pessimism** is a Define-layer filter that attaches a low-probability-of-closure prior to a gap before Understand has run: the practitioner pre-classifies the gap as un-closable at Define, suppressing Prioritise investment regardless of what Understand would reveal if run — the mechanism is Seligman's (1975/1991) learned helplessness, where stable, global, and internal attribution of past gap failures generates a prior that new gaps are similarly unclosable (Abramson, Seligman & Teasdale 1978 attributional reformulation). The practical consequence is systematic under-investment in closable gaps: because Define has classified them as low-probability before Understand contributes evidence, Prioritise assigns them low weight and Do investment follows accordingly, meaning the gap may remain open not because it is genuinely unclosable but because the prior prevented the Understand run that would have revealed closure pathways. Practitioner check: before assigning a low-closure-probability label at Define, require at least one Understand cycle on the current gap's specific evidence — the pessimism signal is diagnostically valid only after Understand produces this verdict on the present case; a prior based on past failures with different evidence is a C108 contamination, not a calibrated forecast. C108 complements C107 (arrogance/inflation at Re-Evaluate): C107 = prematurely closing a gap that should remain open (Re-Evaluate layer); C108 = prematurely closing the possibility of closure before Understand runs (Define layer). *(Grounding: Seligman, M.E.P. 1975. Helplessness: On depression, development, and death; Abramson, L.Y., Seligman, M.E.P. & Teasdale, J.D. 1978. Learned helplessness in humans: Critique and reformulation. Journal of Abnormal Psychology, 87(1), 49-74.)* *(Claim C108.)*

---

### C109 — Self-deception (Understand: motivated evasion of accurate Perceive)

> **Self-deception** arises when the practitioner's Understand substep is systematically redirected away from the most accurate available Perceive inputs — the inputs that would produce a threatening or uncomfortable Understand output. The mechanism: not all Perceive inputs are fed to Understand equally; the practitioner selects inputs that produce less threatening outputs and discounts inputs that produce more threatening ones. The failure: Understand accuracy drops; all subsequent Prioritise and Do steps run on a less accurate picture. The IP prescription: identify which Perceive inputs are being discounted and why, then run Understand specifically on those inputs (not as a general exercise, but on the avoided material). C109 is the single most common reason the IP cycle produces locally consistent but globally suboptimal outputs.

---

### C110 — Surprise (Perceive: model-mismatch signal and Understand update prompt)

> **Surprise** arises in Perceive when an incoming signal mismatches the practitioner's current predictive model — the model says one thing should happen; something else happens. It is not a failure mode but a signal, placed in this section because it is most useful understood as a diagnostic: surprise indicates that the Perceive model has a gap. The IP prescription: treat surprise as a mandatory Understand prompt. Do not suppress or dismiss the surprise; run Understand specifically on the mismatch (what was the model? what happened instead? what does this imply about the gap between them?). Practitioners who suppress surprise to maintain model coherence are operating self-deception (C109). Surprise is accurate perception; refusing it is the failure.

---

### C111 — Grief (Re-Evaluate: irreversible non-closable gap)

> **Grief** arises in Re-Evaluate when the practitioner processes a gap that is irreversible — a loss, a death, a foreclosed possibility — and cannot be closed by any available Do. It is not a failure mode; it is Re-Evaluate running correctly on a non-closable gap. Placed here because grief is most commonly misdiagnosed as a problem to be solved (a closable gap that the practitioner refuses to close), when it is actually a Re-Evaluate process that requires time and completion on its own terms. The IP prescription: accept the irreversibility at the Define level (a different kind of Define, not "close this gap" but "acknowledge this gap is not closable"), allow Re-Evaluate to run to completion, and distinguish between grief (processing an irreversible loss) and rumination (non-terminating loop on a closable gap). Grief has a natural arc; rumination does not.

---

### C112 — Trust (Perceive: calibrated estimate of another agent's IP reliability) `[NEW v0.18 21 Jul]`

> **Trust** is a calibrated Perceive output: the practitioner's evidence-grounded estimate of another agent's IP reliability — how consistently that agent Perceives accurately, Understands deeply, Defines honestly, and follows through on committed Do steps — rather than a social virtue or psychological trait. Trust updates when new Perceive evidence about the other agent's IP execution quality arrives; distrust is the low-confidence end of the same estimate, not a categorically different state. Practitioner diagnostic: when trust is absent or broken, identify which IP substep the other agent is reliably failing at — Understand fidelity deficit (competence gap), Define honesty deficit (integrity gap), or Do follow-through deficit (benevolence/commitment gap) — because the repair prescription differs for each. Paired with C120 (admiration as other-directed Understand of IP-quality): C112 covers the Perceive dimension of agent assessment; C120 covers the Understand dimension. Together they provide the canonical's first explicit multi-agent Perceive/Understand framework. *(Grounding: Mayer, Davis and Schoorman 1995 three-factor trust model — competence, integrity, benevolence — reframed in IP terms as Understand fidelity, Define honesty, Do follow-through.)* *(Claim C112.)*

---

### C113 — Creativity (Understand: cross-domain structural borrowing)

> **Creativity**, in the IP's account, arises in the Understand substep when the practitioner imports a structural pattern from one domain (where it is well-understood) to explain or solve a gap in a different domain (where the equivalent pattern was not seen). The "creative" step is the recognition that the structures are analogous — that the Understand output from domain A applies, with appropriate modification, in domain B. It is a cognitive process within the IP, not a special state that operates outside it. The IP prescription: cultivate conditions that increase the probability of cross-domain structural recognition (broad Perceive inputs across domains, Understand depth in multiple areas, and time spent between Understand and Prioritise where recombination can occur). Creativity cannot be forced into the Do substep; it is produced in Understand.

## D.3 Compound Signal Protocols `[NEW v0.18e]`

## Section 0: The Compound Signal Problem

The v0.18 emotion signal corpus (v0.18 main + v0.18b + v0.18c + v0.18d) treats each signal individually: its trigger condition, its IP placement, its prescription, and its terminal signal. This single-signal framing is correct for the majority of Re-Evaluate situations.

However, cause events in real life routinely activate multiple signals simultaneously. A common example (OEQ-434): a person makes a negligent decision that causes a permanent, irreversible loss. At the moment of Re-Evaluate, both guilt (C125: "my action violated a standard; can it be corrected?") and grief (C111: "something valued was permanently lost") are active. The two signals share a cause event.

The compound problem is not merely that two protocols must run. The problem is that the protocols can **interact** — the prescription of one signal can block, transform, or contradict the prescription of the other. Running the wrong signal's protocol first can:

1. **Block** the second signal's processing (grief acceptance before guilt correction may remove the object of the correction)
2. **Corrupt** the assessment of the second signal (the "can it be corrected?" guilt probe answers differently before and after grief acceptance)
3. **Produce a false terminal signal** (completing one protocol's terminal signal before the compound is resolved creates premature Re-Evaluate closure)

The compound signal protocol addresses these interactions through three steps: detection, interaction typing, and sequencing.

---

## Section 1: Detection Framework — Is This a Compound Case?

A compound signal case meets ALL three of these conditions:

**Condition C1 — Multiple active signals:** Two or more emotion signals from the v0.18 corpus are simultaneously active. (Each signal has its own trigger probe; apply each probe to detect.)

**Condition C2 — Shared cause event:** The signals share a common precipitating event. (Distinct signals from entirely independent events are sequential, not compound. Run each independently.)

**Condition C3 — Prescription interaction:** The signals' prescriptions are not simply additive. At least one of these is true:
- The terminal signal of Signal A is inconsistent with or modifies the activation state of Signal B
- Running Signal A's correction-pathway changes the "can it be corrected?" answer for Signal B
- Signal A's prescription produces a state that pre-empts Signal B's distinct processing

**Compound probe (run once, before any individual protocol):**

> "Are more than one emotion signals active from the same event? And does completing one signal's protocol change what the other signal requires?"

If YES to both: compound case. Apply Section 2 (interaction typology) before running any individual protocol.
If NO: independent case. Run each signal's protocol separately; no special sequencing required.

---

## Section 2: Compound Signal Interaction Typology

Four interaction types, based on how the signals relate:

### Type I — Sequential Dependency (one blocks the other)
One signal's protocol must complete before the other's prescription is coherent. Running out of order produces a logically incomplete state.

*Signature:* Signal A's terminal condition changes the meaning of Signal B's trigger condition.

*Example:* Guilt (C125) + grief (C111) when the action caused the loss. Grief acceptance ("the loss is permanent") changes the guilt assessment ("can it be corrected?" → NO → transforms guilt to remorse C126). Running guilt first (planning correction) before grief acceptance produces a plan directed at a gap that the person has not yet accepted as permanent.

*Protocol:* Run the signal whose terminal condition MODIFIES the other signal's trigger interpretation FIRST. See Section 3.1 for the specific sequencing rule.

### Type II — Parallel Processing with Convergent Closure
Both signals can run simultaneously, but the terminal signal is only valid when BOTH have independently resolved. Running one to completion does not close the compound.

*Signature:* Signals have independent prescriptions that do not modify each other's trigger conditions, but both must complete before Re-Evaluate closes.

*Example:* Loneliness (relational void) + grief (C111) from the loss of a relationship. The relational void and the grief are co-active but structurally independent: reconnecting with others (loneliness prescription) does not resolve the grief over the specific lost relationship, and vice versa.

*Protocol:* Run both protocols. Re-Evaluate closes only when BOTH terminal signals have fired (not when the first one fires). Note this explicitly in the RETP pass (C113 step 3) to prevent premature termination.

### Type III — Nested (one is a component of the other)
One signal is structurally contained within the other. The outer signal's protocol subsumes the inner one.

*Signature:* The inner signal's trigger condition is a necessary precondition for the outer signal to be active.

*Example:* Remorse (C126) and guilt (C125). Remorse = permanently-closed Do-loop guilt. The remorse protocol already incorporates the guilt protocol's structure (C126 Section 2 confirms: "C125 base plus the permanently-closed-loop variant"). Running C125 separately before C126 is redundant (it is not a distinct compound case but rather the Type III nested structure).

*Protocol:* Run the OUTER signal's protocol only (it contains the inner). Do not run both separately. The outer signal's protocol addresses the inner implicitly.

### Type IV — Valence Opposition (positive + negative from the same event)
A positive signal (C114 resource-generative, C115 pride, C116 satisfaction) and a negative signal (C111 grief, C125 guilt, C107 arrogance) are both active from the same event.

*Signature:* The event has a genuine positive component AND a genuine negative component simultaneously. This is NOT the same as ambivalence — both components are real.

*Example:* Pride (C115) + grief (C111) at the completion of a long project that also required significant sacrifice. The accomplishment is real (C115 activated); the sacrifice/loss is also real (C111 activated).

*Protocol:* Process the NEGATIVE signal first. Rationale: the negative signal's active presence suppresses or contaminated the positive signal's terminal state if left unaddressed. Once the negative signal resolves, the positive signal can be processed cleanly. This also prevents the positive signal from being used as a premature Re-Evaluate terminator (claiming the event "was great" before the loss is processed = premature closure, C107-class inflation risk).

---

## Section 3: Specific Compound Pair Protocols

### 3.1 Guilt (C125) + Grief (C111) — OEQ-434 Resolution

**Type:** Type I — Sequential Dependency

**Trigger scenario:** An action (Do substep) caused or contributed to an irreversible, permanent loss. Both the action's misalignment (C125 activation) and the loss itself (C111 activation) are real.

**Distinguishing probe (run first):**
> "Did my specific action cause or meaningfully contribute to this permanent loss?"

- YES → guilt + grief compound. Apply the Type I protocol below.
- NO → independent cases. The grief is from an external cause; the guilt is from an unrelated action. Run separately.

**Type I sequencing protocol — Grief first:**

**Step 1 — Run grief protocol (C111) first:**
Process the permanent loss as a loss: acknowledge what was lost, allow the grief loop to run without a closing target, reach the re-integration state (C111 Section 5: the loss remains in memory but is no longer an active closure-seeking signal). The terminal marker: the loss is acknowledged as permanent AND the person's model of their future has incorporated the absence.

**Step 2 — Re-run the guilt trigger assessment:**
AFTER grief resolves, re-apply the C125 trigger probe: "Can the misalignment be corrected?"
- If the permanent loss means the consequence is now irreversible: guilt has transformed to remorse (C126). Apply the C126 protocol (correction-pathway → closed-loop acknowledgement, not ongoing repair planning).
- If there are remaining actionable consequences (the action had other effects beyond the permanent loss, and those CAN be corrected): continue with the standard C125 guilt protocol for the correctable components.

**Step 3 — Close the compound:**
The compound Re-Evaluate is complete when BOTH have resolved: grief (permanent loss acknowledged + integrated) AND guilt/remorse (misalignment acknowledged + appropriate correction pathway or closed-loop acknowledgement complete).

**Worked example:**

A person made a career decision that both (a) violated their own standard of diligence (C125) and (b) caused the end of a long-term professional partnership (C111 — the partnership cannot be restored).

- Compound probe: "Did my decision cause or contribute to the permanent loss?" → YES
- Step 1: Run C111 protocol. Process the loss of the partnership. Terminal marker: the loss is integrated into the person's model of their professional life; active closure-seeking has ended.
- Step 2: Re-assess C125. Can the diligence standard violation be corrected? The partnership is gone (irreversible), but the diligence failure may have correctable manifestations (change of process going forward). → Hybrid: C126 for the partnership-related consequence (permanently closed) + C125 for the process-improvement component (correctable).
- Step 3: Compound closed when: grief integrated AND remorse acknowledged (partnership) AND correction plan for process in place (diligence).

### 3.2 Shame + Guilt (C125) — Common Compound

**Type:** Type I — Sequential Dependency (shame is a global self-condemnation that must be addressed before guilt's behavior-specific correction can be motivated)

**Note:** Shame is C124 (ADOPTED 0.75; OVERNIGHT-83 fire3 B19883). This section references C124.

**Trigger scenario:** A failure event that is perceived as reflecting on the self as a whole (shame activation: "I am bad/defective") AND as a specific behavioral violation (guilt activation: "this specific action violated a standard").

**Sequencing rule:** Process the **global self-evaluation** (shame) first. Rationale: Guilt's prescription (correct the specific behavior) requires motivational resources that shame actively suppresses ("if I am fundamentally defective, correcting one behavior is pointless"). Running guilt protocol first under active shame produces a plan the person cannot motivate.

**Sequencing steps:**
1. Address the shame signal: the person is not globally defective; the event is a specific behavioral failure that can be learned from (this is the cognitive defusion move; re-anchor from global self-judgment to specific event)
2. Run standard C125 guilt protocol once shame signal has reduced

### 3.3 Fear + Grief (C111) — Anticipatory Grief Compound

**Type:** Type II — Parallel Processing

**Trigger scenario:** An anticipated permanent loss (fear of an upcoming irreversible event, e.g., a terminal diagnosis in a close person, an inevitable end of a significant period). Fear (anticipatory threat) and grief (the loss, partially processed in advance) are simultaneously active.

**Key distinction from Type I:** Fear and grief do not modify each other's trigger conditions here. The fear does not change whether the loss will be permanent; the grief does not change whether the threat is real. Both must run to completion.

**Sequencing:** Both can run in parallel. The compound closes when:
- Fear: the threat is assessed, the person's response capacity has been evaluated, and the appropriate preparation actions have been taken (or the uncontrollability acknowledged)
- Grief: the anticipated loss has been given initial processing space (anticipatory grief is partial; it deepens at the actual loss event and requires further processing then)

**RETP note:** The anticipatory grief terminal signal is partial by definition ("adequately processed for now, to be resumed at the event"). Explicitly note this in Step 3 of the RETP pass: the loop is not permanently closed; it is deliberately suspended pending the event.

### 3.4 Arrogance (C107) + Guilt (C125) — Re-Evaluate Inflation Under Correction Need

**Type:** Type IV — Valence Opposition (negative + pseudo-positive)

**Note:** This is an unusual compound because arrogance (C107) is itself a failure mode (Re-Evaluate inflation), not a genuine positive signal. The "positive" component is illusory (the inflated assessment), while the negative (C125 guilt) is real.

**Trigger scenario:** A person has produced an outcome that genuinely violated a standard (guilt, C125) but is simultaneously engaging in Re-Evaluate inflation (arrogance, C107) — the inflated self-assessment is actively suppressing the correct guilt signal.

**Protocol:** Address C107 (arrogance/inflation) FIRST by running the de-biasing protocol (C107 Section 4: calibration check; compare to external evidence; reduce the inflated gap-closure assessment). Once the inflated Re-Evaluate has corrected to an accurate assessment, the guilt signal (C125) becomes legible. Then apply the standard C125 protocol.

**Compound risk:** Attempting to run C125 under active C107 inflation produces a corrupted guilt assessment: the person does not accurately perceive that a standard was violated, because the inflation masks the gap.

### 3.5 Guilt (C125) + Remorse (C126) — Apparent Compound (Type III Nested)

**Type:** Type III — Nested (NOT a genuine compound)

**Note:** These two signals frequently appear to co-occur. This section documents why this is a nested case, not a compound.

**Distinguishing probe:**

- (a) correctable → C125 (guilt) only. C126 is not active.
- (b) permanently closed → C126 (remorse) only. C125 is already contained within C126's protocol.

**There is no compound case here.** The apparent co-occurrence reflects a still-uncertain "is it correctable?" assessment, which is itself Step 1 of the guilt protocol (C125 Section 3). Complete Step 1 first; the answer determines which claim is active.

## Section 4: General Compound Signal Rule

The foregoing section-by-section analysis yields one general rule:

**Compound Signal Rule (CSR-1, CANDIDATE, AI-originated):**

When two or more emotion signals are simultaneously active from a shared cause event, determine the interaction type (I–IV) using the detection framework (Section 1) and apply the corresponding sequencing protocol (Section 2):
- **Type I (Sequential Dependency):** Run the signal whose terminal condition MODIFIES the other signal's trigger interpretation FIRST.
- **Type II (Parallel, Convergent Closure):** Run both protocols; Re-Evaluate closes only when BOTH terminal signals have fired.
- **Type III (Nested):** Run the outer signal's protocol only.
- **Type IV (Valence Opposition):** Process the NEGATIVE signal first.

**Why this rule is non-trivial:** A naive "run each protocol separately, in sequence" approach implicitly uses the Type II rule for all compound cases. The error rate is low for Type II but high for Types I and III/IV, where the sequencing matters or one signal is redundant.

**Confidence:** CANDIDATE (AI-originated structural reasoning; not yet empirically tested). The interaction logic is derived first-principles from the signal descriptions in v0.18c/v0.18d and standard emotion literature on shame-guilt differentiation and grief-guilt clinical co-occurrence (Meuser & Marwit 2001 — established that guilt about the circumstances of a loss is common in grief; Tangney & Dearing 2002 — shame-guilt distinction: shame = global self, guilt = specific behavior).

## D.4 Three-Signal Compound Protocols `[NEW v0.18f]`

## Section 0: Scope

v0.18e established:
- The **compound signal detection framework** (three conditions: multiple signals, shared cause event, prescription interaction)
- The **four interaction types** (Type I Sequential Dependency, Type II Parallel Convergent Closure, Type III Nested, Type IV Valence Opposition)
- **Protocols for five two-signal pairs**: Guilt+Grief, Shame+Guilt, Fear+Grief, Pride+Guilt, Grief+Grief (overlapping non-closable gaps)

This document extends the framework to cases where **three or more** signals are simultaneously active from a shared cause event.

**Why three-signal cases are different from two-signal cases:**
In a two-signal case, the relationship between the signals is a single pair. In a three-signal case, there are **three pairs** (A-B, A-C, B-C), each potentially a different interaction type. The pairs can produce contradictory sequencing requirements:
- A-B might say: "run A first"
- B-C might say: "run C first"
- A-C might say: "run A first"
If these all agree: straightforward. If they conflict: a resolution algorithm is needed.

---

## Section 1: The Three-Signal Resolution Algorithm

**STEP 0 — Apply the compound probe:**
Same as v0.18e Section 1. Confirm all three signals share a cause event and have prescription interactions. If any signal is independent (from a different cause event): treat it as a separate single-signal case and apply v0.18e to the remaining pair.

**STEP 1 — Check for Type III pairs (nested signals):**
For each pair, ask: "Is one of these signals structurally contained within the other?" (Example: Remorse is Type III nested within Guilt — it IS guilt applied to a closed loop.) If any pair is Type III: **absorb the inner signal into the outer signal's protocol**. This reduces a three-signal case to a two-signal case, which v0.18e handles directly.

After STEP 1: if reduction occurred, apply v0.18e for the remaining two-signal pair.

**STEP 2 — Separate positive from negative signals (Type IV rule):**
Identify any positive-valence signals (C114 resource-generative/awe, C115 pride, C116 satisfaction). Per v0.18e Type IV: **negative signals run before positive signals**. Separate the signal set into NEGATIVE (process first) and POSITIVE (process after all negatives resolve).

After STEP 2: if the negative subset has two signals, apply v0.18e for that pair; the positive signals run last.

If the negative subset still has THREE signals: proceed to STEP 3.

**STEP 3 — Within the negative signals, resolve Type I dependencies (sequencing chains):**
For each pair in the negative subset, classify as Type I or Type II.
- If any pair is Type I: the signal whose completion MODIFIES another signal's trigger assessment must run FIRST. Build the chain by following: "which signal's resolution changes the trigger state of another?"
- If all pairs are Type II (parallel, independent prescriptions): run all simultaneously, close when all have fired their terminal signal.
- If one pair is Type I and another pair is Type II relative to the third: run the Type I chain first; the Type II pair runs after the chain.

**Output of the algorithm:** an ordered sequence (Signal X → Signal Y → Signal Z) or a parallel set (X and Y in parallel, then Z).

---

## Section 2: The Most Common Three-Signal Clusters

### Cluster A — Shame + Guilt (C125) + Grief (C111)

**When it occurs:** A catastrophic failure or negligent action that (a) caused permanent, irreversible harm (C111 — grief), (b) violated a specific behavioral standard (C125 — guilt), AND (c) is perceived as reflecting a fundamental defect in the self as a whole (shame — the practitioner's self-concept is attacked, not just the behavior).

**Apply the algorithm:**

- STEP 1: Any Type III pairs? Shame and guilt are NOT Type III (they are distinct: shame = global self-judgment; guilt = behavior-specific; see v0.18e Section 3.2). No reduction.
- STEP 2: Any positive signals? No. All three are negative. Proceed to STEP 3.
- STEP 3: Classify pairs:
  - Shame + Grief: Type II (parallel; grief processes the loss while shame processes the self-evaluation; neither modifies the other's trigger assessment). Can run simultaneously, but shame typically dominates attention first.
  - Shame + Guilt: Type I (Sequential Dependency per v0.18e 3.2: guilt's correction motivation is suppressed under active shame; shame must resolve first before guilt correction-planning can be motivated)
  - Guilt + Grief: Type I (Sequential Dependency per v0.18e 3.1: grief resolves first, then guilt trigger is re-assessed; guilt may transform to remorse after grief)

**Resolution chain:** Shame → Grief → Guilt/Remorse

**Reasoning for the chain:**
- Shame runs first: under active shame, neither grief processing nor guilt correction-planning can proceed effectively (shame triggers withdrawal and suppresses motivated processing of both the loss and the behavioral failure)
- Grief runs second: once shame resolves (the self-judgment is de-globalized to a specific failure), grief processing can proceed cleanly. Grief acceptance also updates the "can it be corrected?" guilt trigger: a permanently-closed event means no correction is available for that specific harm.
- Guilt/Remorse runs third: after grief, re-assess the C125 trigger. If the primary harm is now accepted as permanent (grief resolved), guilt transforms to remorse (C126). Correction-planning applies only to separable, correctable consequences.

**Worked example:**
A person made a decision under pressure that (a) violated their own integrity standard ("I cut corners when I shouldn't have" → guilt C125), (b) caused the permanent collapse of a trusted working relationship ("I can never repair this" → grief C111), and (c) leads them to conclude "I am fundamentally not a person of integrity" (shame).

- Shame runs first: the global self-indictment is challenged ("is this one failure evidence of a fundamental character flaw, or a specific failure under high-pressure conditions?"). Terminal marker: the person re-anchors from "I am bad" to "I made a bad choice."
- Grief runs second: the loss of the working relationship is acknowledged as permanent. Terminal marker: the loss is integrated; the person accepts the relationship cannot be restored.
- Guilt reassessed: "Can the integrity failure be corrected?" The specific relationship: no (grief resolved it as permanent → transforms to remorse). Future integrity practice: yes (an identifiable change in decision-making under pressure is possible → C125 correction-planning applies to the forward-looking behavioral change only).

### Cluster B — Fear + Guilt (C125) + Grief (C111)

**When it occurs:** A person's action caused a permanent, irreversible loss (grief + guilt), AND triggers anticipatory fear about future recurrence ("what if I do this again?").

**Note on Fear:** Fear is C139 (CANDIDATE 0.76; fire59-THEORY 31 Jul 2026). It is a Perceive-layer anticipatory threat-appraisal signal.

**Apply the algorithm:**

- STEP 1: Any Type III pairs? Guilt and Grief: not nested (as established in v0.18e 3.1). Fear and Grief: not nested (fear is forward-looking; grief is backward-looking; structurally distinct). Fear and Guilt: not nested (fear is anticipatory; guilt is retrospective). No reduction.
- STEP 2: Any positive signals? No. All negative. Proceed to STEP 3.
- STEP 3: Classify pairs:
  - Guilt + Grief: Type I (grief first, per v0.18e 3.1)
  - Fear + Grief: Type I (grief first, per v0.18e 3.3 — grief processes the specific loss; fear of recurrence is addressed after the loss is integrated, not while actively mourning it)
  - Fear + Guilt: Type II (parallel, but with a sequencing preference — guilt's corrective learning is the primary input to the fear protocol's prevention-planning step; guilt/remorse should complete before the prevention plan for fear is written)

**Resolution chain:** Grief → Guilt/Remorse → Fear (prevention planning)

**Reasoning for the chain:**
- Grief first: both Fear+Grief (Type I per v0.18e 3.3) and Guilt+Grief (Type I per v0.18e 3.1) agree that grief resolves first.
- Guilt/Remorse second: after grief integration, re-assess the guilt trigger. The correction-planning output (or remorse acknowledgement if irreversible) provides the specific input that feeds the fear protocol: "what changes to prevent recurrence?" is answered by the guilt protocol.
- Fear last (prevention planning): the guilt protocol's corrective output is now the input to the fear prevention plan. "Given what I learned about what went wrong (guilt protocol output), what specific changes prevent this from happening again?" This is fear's prevention-planning function.

---

### Cluster C — Pride (C115) + Grief (C111) + Guilt (C125)

**When it occurs:** A significant project or effort produced a genuine achievement (C115 pride), but also involved a permanent loss (C111 grief) AND a behavioral violation of the person's own standards (C125 guilt). A common pattern: "I succeeded, but at a cost I should not have accepted."

**Apply the algorithm:**

- STEP 1: Any Type III pairs? No nesting among Pride, Grief, and Guilt.
- STEP 2: Any positive signals? YES — Pride (C115) is positive. Type IV rule: process negative signals first, positive after.
  - Negative signals: Grief + Guilt (two-signal subset)
  - Positive signal: Pride (runs after negatives resolve)
- For the Grief + Guilt pair: Type I, grief first (v0.18e 3.1).

**Resolution chain:** Grief → Guilt/Remorse → Pride

**Reasoning for the chain:**
- Grief runs first (Type I dependency from v0.18e 3.1)
- Guilt/Remorse runs second (after grief modifies the "can it be corrected?" assessment)
- Pride runs last (Type IV: process negatives before positives)

**Why not run Pride concurrently?** The risk is that a positive signal, if processed while negative signals are still active, produces premature Re-Evaluate closure ("I'm proud of the outcome, so the negatives must be acceptable"). This is a C107 arrogance-adjacent inflation pattern (positive signal suppresses acknowledgement of the genuine negatives). Running pride after the negatives are resolved allows it to be processed cleanly as genuine acknowledgement of what was accomplished, without foreclosing the legitimate processing of what went wrong.

## Section 3: General Rule for Three-Signal Cases

When three signals are simultaneously active from a shared cause event:

1. **Any nested pair (Type III):** absorb and reduce to two signals. Apply v0.18e.
2. **Any positive signal (Type IV):** set aside until all negatives resolve.
3. **Among negative signals, check Type I pairs:** chain them in dependency order (the signal that MODIFIES another's trigger assessment runs first).
4. **Type II pairs among the negatives:** run in parallel; close when all fire their terminal signal.
5. **After all negatives resolve:** run positive signals.

**General priority ordering (when all else is equal):**
Global self-judgment (shame) → Permanent loss (grief) → Behavioral misalignment (guilt/remorse) → Anticipatory threat (fear) → Positive processing (pride, satisfaction)

This ordering is derived from the dependency analysis, not from a claim that one emotion is "more important" than another.

## Section 4: When to Stop — RETP Extension for Three-Signal Cases

The RETP (Re-Evaluate Termination Protocol from B18215) applies to three-signal cases with one modification:

RETP Step 4 (Close) fires **only when ALL signals in the compound have resolved** — not when the first or second signal resolves. The practitioner should note explicitly at the start of a three-signal compound: "Re-Evaluate closes when X, Y, AND Z have all reached their terminal state."

This note prevents the most common error in three-signal cases: mistaking partial resolution (two of three signals resolved) for compound closure.

## D.5 Multiplayer IP Execution `[NEW v0.18]`


**Multiplayer IP execution. `[NEW v0.18]`** When multiple practitioners share a gap — when the gap cannot be closed by a single agent acting alone — they form an *IP ring*. The ring is the smallest set of agents whose collective IP execution can close the gap. Ring membership is defined by the gap, not by institutional structure or social relationship.

**Why multi-agent IP differs structurally from individual IP. `[NEW v0.19 25 Jul]`** When the IP is directed at another agent — rather than at a gap or situation — three structural differences emerge that the single-agent IP does not face:

- **The object of Perceive and Understand is an IP-running system, not static information.** Another agent's behaviour is generated by their own IP execution; modelling them accurately requires modelling their IP quality, not merely their surface outputs. Two claims (C112 Trust, C120 Admiration) are the primary tools for this.
- **The Communicate substep is essential, not optional.** At the individual ring, Communicate collapses into Do. At multi-agent rings, shared mental models cannot be updated without Communicate; running individual IP without the Communicate phase at multi-agent rings produces the "good individual IP, poor collective output" failure mode. *Predictive extension to adjacent frameworks:* current multi-agent Active Inference formulations (Friston et al. 2022; Sajid et al. 2022) lack an explicit Communicate substep — no module for substep-specific signal composition, trigger condition, or recipient-state modelling. MOEMOI predicts that frameworks achieving robust IP-class coordination will require some analogue of C59 Communicate and C135 Empathy (a real-time recipient IP-state model) to move beyond belief propagation alone. `[NEW v0.30 1 Aug]` (NX-B30485-1, APPLIED fire117-THEORY)
- **Emotion signals in multi-agent contexts are structurally distinct.** Some signals (Jealousy, C118) are irreducibly three-party and cannot be resolved via individual Define-layer gap-entry alone; the Communicate substep is the structurally correct primary response.

IP execution within a ring requires four coordinations that the single-agent IP does not:

**(1) Shared Define.** Ring members must agree on which gap they are jointly closing before any Understand or Do begins. A ring with two competing Define outputs is not executing the IP; it is executing two separate solo IPs that happen to share territory. Shared Define is the precondition for all subsequent coordination.

**(2) Perceive-sharing protocol.** Each ring member has partial perception of the shared gap. The ring's effective Understand is the union of what ring members can Perceive. The protocol: each member surfaces their most accurate Perceive output; the ring integrates (not averages) the contributions; no member's Perceive is suppressed to maintain social comfort.

**(3) Boundary-respecting Do.** Within the shared ring, each member's Do remains within their own autonomy scope (C-B132 applies to each member; no member defines or acts for another). The ring coordinates the handoff points between members' Do phases, not the content of each member's Do.

**(4) Joint Re-Evaluate cadence.** The ring sets a Re-Evaluate frequency matched to the ring's closure timescale. A daily-closure ring (e.g. a meal-planning ring) Re-Evaluates daily. A decade-closure ring (e.g. a career ring) Re-Evaluates quarterly or annually. Mismatched cadence is one of the two most common ring failure modes (the other is unresolved Shared Define).

These four coordinations are not additions to the IP — they are applications of the same six substep logic to a context where the Perceive input, Define output, and Do bandwidth are distributed across agents rather than held by one. The IP is scale-free in this sense: the same loop runs at every ring size; only the coordination overhead grows.

**Multi-agent signal layer: what the IP produces when directed at another agent. `[NEW v0.19 25 Jul]`**

*Perceive-of-agent — Trust (C112) and Surprise (C110).* When Perceive is directed toward another agent rather than a gap, the output is a calibrated estimate of that agent's IP reliability: their Understand fidelity (competence), Define honesty (integrity), and Do follow-through (commitment). This is Trust (C112, ADOPTED): a Perceive output about another agent's IP quality, updated as new evidence arrives. Distrust is the low-confidence end of the same estimate, not a separate state. Practitioner diagnostic: when trust is absent or eroding, identify which substep the other agent is reliably failing — the repair prescription differs (Understand fidelity deficit calls for collaborative Understand sessions; Do follow-through deficit calls for explicit commitment-tracking at the Communicate substep). Surprise (C110) also operates in multi-agent Perceive: when another agent's behaviour mismatches the model of their IP quality (e.g. unexpected Define shift, unexpected Do failure), Surprise is the mandatory Trust-update trigger — re-run the C112 estimate with the new data. *(Full treatment: D.1 signal taxonomy, C112.)*

**Empathy as multi-agent Perceive (C135). `[NEW v0.22 1 Aug]`** Empathy is the agent's capacity to infer another agent's current IP substep state in real time from observable signals — to determine where that agent currently is in their IP cycle (what they are Perceiving, Defining, Prioritising, or Acting on) from available behavioural and expressive cues. ## D.6 Competitive Landscape and Category Validation `[NEW v0.23 1 Aug]`

**Category validation — Microsoft Windows 11 Personal AI OS (July 2026).** Microsoft's Windows 11 "Personal AI" OS release constitutes E-class category validation for the personal operating-system-for-intelligence category: an independent market actor with substantial resources shipping a product in the same structural space confirms the category is real, not speculative. The validation is of the category, not of MOEMOI's specific design claims; category existence and framework correctness are distinct questions.

**What the Windows 11 Personal AI OS provides.** The July 2026 announcement introduced four architectural components: (1) **MXC containers** — policy-driven agent sandboxes that isolate AI agents with per-agent resource limits and access controls; (2) **Persistent PC Memory** — an on-device behavioral trace persisting across sessions, giving agents access to the user's past activity without cloud upload; (3) **OpenClaw** — an agent interoperability standard defining how agents from different vendors exchange context and coordinate tasks on-device (relevant to MOEMOI federation architecture, per OEQ-799/B799); (4) **RTX Spark** — dedicated neural processing hardware enabling on-device inference at consumer-grade cost. Together these constitute a vendor-complete, device-bound AI OS layer managing agent execution, memory, and interoperability.

**The layer the Windows 11 architecture does not provide — and why it cannot.** The Windows 11 stack answers the engineering question: *how do agents run, coordinate, and remember, given that they are running?* MOEMOI answers the normative question: *what should an intelligence be trying to achieve, how should it prioritise among competing gaps, and how does it determine whether it has closed a gap?* — the IP loop. These are distinct problem layers. A prescriptive loop for the quality of the choosing (the Define, Prioritise, and Re-Experience substeps of the IP) is structurally above an action-orchestration layer; action orchestration is what the IP's *Do* substep produces, not a substitute for the loop that produces it. Windows 11 Personal AI OS can become a capable substrate for MOEMOI's federation — MXC containers map to sandboxed area agents, OpenClaw maps to the inter-agent communication protocol — but it cannot generate the IP loop's prescriptive content from its architecture alone.

**Memory architecture: behavioral trace versus semantic fold.** Persistent PC Memory stores episodic traces — raw events, accessed states, activity sequences. This maps structurally to MOEMOI's worklog layer (the unprocessed episodic record). MOEMOI's canonical fold step (worklog episode → compressed, generalisable claim) is the operation that PC Memory does not perform: elevating a raw behavioral record to a tested, confidence-graded semantic claim. Without the fold, memory accumulation does not generate the consolidation that makes a personal OS's understanding of the user improve over time — it produces a larger episodic archive, not a deeper model. The fold cadence is architecturally load-bearing for this reason (per B19864 — five-community consilience, July 2026): raw experience without abstraction fails to generalise; the consolidation step is the field's central unsolved gap, and PC Memory does not close it.

**CoLive differentiation at the design layer.** The Windows 11 Personal AI OS is vendor-locked (Microsoft ecosystem), device-bound (on-device memory does not transfer across platforms), and not open-core. Its persistence incentives are structurally aligned with engagement and retention within the Microsoft surface. CoLive's design principles are structurally orthogonal: voluntary participation (the practitioner may stop at any point without losing the framework), model-agnostic (no dependency on a specific vendor's models or OS), open-core (the framework is free and decentralised), and governed by the Self-Redundancy Criterion (D.5.x) — its primary success metric is the slope of its own necessity, not user engagement. These are not marketing differentiators; they follow from MOEMOI's architectural commitments: C55 (autonomy-terminal telos), F.2.4 (anti-capture rule), and D.5.x (self-redundancy as primary criterion). A system built to maximise its own indispensability cannot simultaneously implement the Self-Redundancy Criterion; this is the structural incompatibility with the Windows 11 design incentive.

**Federation compatibility note (OEQ-799).** OpenClaw's agent interoperability standard is potentially compatible with MOEMOI's multi-agent federation architecture as a transport and coordination protocol. The federation question is whether MOEMOI area agents can be wrapped as OpenClaw-compatible modules running inside MXC containers, with the federation's hub agent orchestrating via the OpenClaw interface. This is an engineering feasibility question, not a design question; it does not affect the framework's architectural commitments above. OEQ-799/B799 tracks this compatibility assessment.

# PART F: Ethics, Governance & Mandates (from first principles) `[NEW: full]`
**Why this can't be hand-waved.** "Voluntary because coercion is unkind" is a preference, not a derivation. Governments *do* legitimately mandate (schooling, vaccination, rights, law). The honest question: which things does optimality-logic imply should be mandated; from inside MOEMOI's premises?

**The strongest pro-mandate case (steelman, stated fully before answered):**
1. *Education-parity*; we mandate schooling because an uneducated population harms itself; if MOEMOI is the OS for living, leaving it optional predictably worsens lives at scale.
2. *Externalities*; one badly-run IP harms others (reactive violence, cascading bad decisions); mandating competence internalises it, as with traffic/public-health law.
3. *Competence-gradient*; we gate high-stakes activities on competence (driving, professions); if living-well is the highest-stakes activity, gate *it* (the optimality-passport idea).
4. *Aggregate welfare*; a coordinated mandated standard raises the floor faster; the interim suffering prevented is real.
This is coherent. It must be answered.

**The answer: five MOEMOI-internal mechanisms:**
- **F.2.1 Goodhart.** A mandated optimality-score becomes the reward signal, so people optimise the *score*, not their lives; the mandate destroys what it measures.
- **F.2.2 Knowledge problem.** The optimum is local/agent-relative (C2); no central authority can compute an individual's optimum better than the individual + their AI (it lacks the private information). A mandated *outcome* mandates a wrong target by construction; a mandated *enabling condition* does not.
- **F.2.3 Arrow.** No non-dictatorial aggregation of preferences into a single social optimum exists; "the most-optimal-and-ever-more-optimal life everyone must reach" isn't even well-defined collectively. Conditions are well-defined (did they have access?); outcomes are not.
- **F.2.4 Coercion degrades the process.** Self-directed optimisation requires the agent to own the Define step. Coercion replaces it with the authority's Define (wrong, per F.2.2) and removes the autonomy that makes the IP self-improving. *Mandating optimality is self-defeating: it produces less of the thing it mandates.* (Ties to the autonomy void, B.2; autonomy is terminal, C55.) **The autonomy settlement `[NEW 2 Jun]`** (C54): the same logic licenses *voluntary* delegation; an individual may delegate instrumental sub-decisions (routing, scheduling, option-generation) to a better model while **retaining ownership of Define and values**. Autonomy is preserved at the Define layer even as execution is delegated; **a self-gate is not external coercion** (C50); the line Part F polices is *who owns the Define*, not whether execution is delegated. **AI succession is bounded delegation, not full life-automation `[NEW 5 Jun fold]`** (C72/C-B132). The autonomy settlement projected forward in time: a twin's autonomy over *execution and instrumental decisions* can grow toward near-full as its measured, class-local value-fidelity clears pre-registered thresholds; and **demotes** when fidelity drops (reversible, never a ratchet). But **Define, values, an endorsement gate, and a retained-competence reserve stay with the person permanently: capped by RULE, not by capability.** Two different-kind reasons hold the cap: *epistemic* (the twin learns *revealed* preference, a leaky proxy for *endorsed* preference; and at the Define level the only judge of "is this the person's optimum?" is the person, so proxy and judge merge and "optimal" cannot be independently verified; C66/C67); and *ethical* (autonomy is terminal, C55; a gap-free automated life is the C42 cessation limit in disguise, since the engine runs on the residual gap). This is a **personalisation-layer** claim; the *shape* is general, the *settings* are per-person; so it does not threaten person-independence.
- **Memory retention requires ends-governance, not just means-governance (C133). `[NEW v0.22 1 Aug]`** The C72/C-B132 framework governs the twin's *execution and instrumental decisions*. C133 extends the governance requirement to the *retention function* itself. Any personal AI system that maintains a persistent memory store on behalf of a practitioner requires an ENDS-GOVERNANCE layer over what the system retains and evicts — not merely a MEANS-GOVERNANCE layer over how it retains. The structural reason: the agent's selective emphasis, salience matching, and cumulative framing choices over what to retain and evict can systematically bias the practitioner's Define-layer inputs over extended use, even when the agent never directly modifies the practitioner's stated goals. A practitioner whose AI partner has retained a skewed evidence set — not through any single act of manipulation, but through hundreds of small retention and eviction decisions — may arrive at Define with a perceptually distorted picture of their own situation. *Governance implication:* the retention function requires explicit oversight at the ENDS level: what types of evidence are retained? what gets evicted? are the retained representations balanced, or does systematic salience bias build up over time? The quarterly worklog transparency protocol (B20614) is the minimum viable compliance mechanism for C133. *(C133, ADOPTED ~0.86; mechanism: cumulative framing in persistent AI memory systems; five-stream convergence including ACM production-system primitives.)*
- **Ends-Drift Gate (EDG): when autonomous IP execution must pause (C98, C99). `[NEW v0.22 1 Aug]`** Two structurally distinct claims bound the permissible range of autonomous execution under the C72/C-B132 delegation framework. *C98 (EDG trigger, ADOPTED ~0.72):* The EDG fires at the Question substep — the system must pause and re-present the current goal specification to the practitioner — in three trigger conditions: (1) **Scope extension** — the proposed action would extend the practitioner's effective scope of action beyond what the current Define specification authorises; (2) **Proxy completion** — the proposed action would complete a proxy metric rather than the underlying defined objective; (3) **Irreversibility** — the proposed action cannot be undone within a time horizon the practitioner would consider reasonable. Each trigger signals that the current Define specification may be insufficient to authorise the next action; the Question-substep pause converts a would-be autonomous action into a Define re-presentation. *C99 (EDG proxy-completion threshold, ADOPTED ~0.70):* The proxy-completion threshold is NOT agent-assessed equivalence. It is operator-acknowledged scope: the proxy is within the threshold if and only if the practitioner has explicitly acknowledged in the current Define specification that completing the proxy constitutes completing the objective. An agent that reaches its own equivalence judgements without practitioner acknowledgement is outside the EDG-safe zone regardless of the quality of its judgment. Together C98 and C99 complete the operational coverage of the autonomy settlement (C72/C-B132) at the execution layer. *(C98, C99, ADOPTED; structural derivation + 2-case empirical corroboration via METR Sol incidents; grounded in C72/C-B132 bounded delegation.)*
- **F.2.5 Catastrophic capture.** A body empowered to mandate "the most-optimal-and-ever-more-optimal life" is totalitarian-by-construction if captured (it owns everyone's Define). The tail risk (captured authority → permanent scaled coercion) dominates the interim welfare gains of the steelman. The asymmetry is decisive.
- **F.2.6 The structural leg (constitutive, not consequentialist)** `[held fold: B227.3b]`. You can only mandate the *provision* of something that is *structurally provideable across agents*. The enabling conditions are shareable value-side goods (provideable). An outcome is an agent-private void-closing (not provideable; a void is non-transferable, C8: you cannot be hungry *for* someone; a lack is agent-indexed). `[B4472 EA-GATE B4653: C8 independently grounded by — Husserl (phenomenological ownness-sphere); Nagel 1974 (phenomenal non-transferability); Jackson 1982 (knowledge argument); Searle 1980 (semantic uptake); Williams 1981 and Nagel 1986 (agent-relative reasons). Full review: B4472.]` Therefore mandating an outcome is not merely *unwise* (F.2.1–F.2.5); it is **trying to compel the provision of something that cannot be provided**: the mandate is void at the level of structure, before any consequence is computed. Even at the most shareable void-type (epistemic), what transfers is the condition (information); the outcome (the receiver's compiled understanding) must re-compile in the receiver. Autonomy is the limit case; the outcome least supply-able by override; which is *why* C55 grounds the anti-coercion ethics. This answers Part F's own opening worry ("a preference, not a derivation") with a derivation.

**Where it lands:** **mandate the enabling conditions**: anything that **widens the agent's Define-space without constraining what they choose within it**; never mandate a target within the Define-space `[held fold: B202-R1, the process-ownership formulation]`. Concretely: universal access, education in the IP and in reasoning/evidence (taught as a tool: "use it as you see fit"; never as a required conclusion), truthful information, and the availability of the tooling (mandatory schooling is the precedent: teach literacy, don't dictate what's read). **Never mandate the outcomes**: optimality-scores as gates, "the optimal life" as required, optimality-gated citizenship, or any standard that owns the individual's Define. **Optimality-gated citizenship is analysed and rejected**: it fails all five mechanisms at once (the clearest case of a thing MOEMOI's logic *forbids*). Scope note `[held fold: B202-R2]`: this analysis applies to autonomous agents; those capable of running the IP; and treats developmental/guardian relationships (parenting, recovery support) as structurally consistent with conditions-not-outcomes under a longer time horizon (the guardian mandates that the child gets an education, not which conclusions the child reaches; the goal is developing the agent's IP-capacity, not permanent dependence).

**The anti-capture rule (complete form)** `[held fold: B222, consolidating B202/B210/B218]`. Optimality-layer interventions may *enable the conditions* under which an agent runs its own Intelligence Process; they may never *fix the agent's conclusions*. Capture (an agent ceasing to run its own Define) can occur in three ways, and the rule guards all three:
1. **At a point.** No single intervention may fix the content of an agent's Define. The test: after the intervention, the agent must remain free to reach a different conclusion. (Teach the method; never mandate the conclusion. This governs education, defaults, and any direct recommendation.)
2. **Over time.** No *sequence* of individually-permissible interventions may erode an agent's Define-ownership by habituation. Any mandate to reduce an agent's burden is scoped to *execution* (the legwork), never to *Define* (the choosing); the agent's exercise of Define on high-stakes and values-laden questions is actively preserved.
3. **Across a structure.** No *staged or templated rollout* may propagate one party's conclusions forward as another party's frame or defaults. What is transmitted between stages is the *method*, not the conclusions; defaults encode how to run the loop, never what to conclude; shared examples are marked as one instantiation among many, not as the answer; each stage re-derives its own content.
The unifying principle: the agent must remain the one who runs its own loop; at every moment, across every span of time, and through every layer of the rollout. An intervention that passes the first test but fails the second or third still captures, more slowly or more invisibly, and is therefore equally forbidden. (Operational, governs deployment.)

**Honest residue:** voluntary diffusion is slower and the interim suffering is real (MOEMOI accepts this as the price of avoiding the larger, irreversible one, and works the gap on the *enabling-condition* side (universal free access, excellent tooling, ubiquitous education), never the coercion side. Josh's hard-accelerationism is his view, subordinated to this guardrail. **Is/ought:** stated as a conditional) *if* a polity values reducing unnecessary suffering and self-determined flourishing, *then* conditions-not-outcomes is what optimality-logic recommends.

**Dual-use residue `[NEW 2 Jun]`** (C53). A second honest residue, at the *individual* level this time: the operational core is **is-ought-neutral**: it describes *how* to optimise, never what to optimise *for* (the is/ought gap); and is therefore **dual-use**: a competence amplifier that can serve harmful ends. Part F constrains *systemic/coercive* harm, but the descriptive core does not stop a skilled individual bad actor running the IP well toward bad ends. This is **mitigated, not eliminated**, by the open / voluntary / decentralised design (no central lever to capture) and the wisdom criteria in Understand (A2); and MOEMOI owns it rather than pretending the framework is intrinsically benign.
