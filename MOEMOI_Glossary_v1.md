# MOEMOI Glossary

*Terms used throughout the framework, preprint, and claims register. Definitions are operational, not metaphysical: they describe how the term is used in MOEMOI, not how it is used in general philosophy of mind or cognitive science.*

---

## Core framework terms

**MOEMOI** (Most Optimal and Ever-More-Optimal Intelligence)
The framework itself. The name encodes the claim: intelligence functions to move continuously toward a more optimal state (not to reach a fixed optimum). The acronym is pronounced "moh-moy."

**Intelligence Process (IP)**
The six-substep loop that MOEMOI proposes all intelligent systems implement when they improve. The IP is the unit of analysis for the entire framework. See EUPDDL.

**EUPDDL**
Acronym for the six substeps of the IP: Experience, Understand, Prioritise, Define, Prepare, and Do/Learn. Each substep is necessary; none is sufficient alone. The framework's central claim is that all improvement, in any intelligent system, can be described as one or more passes through EUPDDL.

- **Experience (E):** Perceive and retrieve relevant information from the environment and memory. The quality of input here propagates through every downstream substep.
- **Understand (U):** Make sense of what was experienced. Identify the real problem, not just the presented one. Weigh evidence, correct reasoning errors.
- **Prioritise (P):** Select which gap to close given limited resources. The highest-leverage substep when multiple valid gaps exist simultaneously.
- **Define (D1):** Specify the target state, success criteria, and constraints precisely enough to act. "Define" here is a technical term; it is not the same as "describe" or "explain."
- **Prepare (P2):** Acquire the resources, knowledge, and conditions needed to close the gap. Preparation is not synonymous with planning; it includes capability acquisition.
- **Do/Learn (DL):** Act on the world and then integrate what happened. The Learn element is the feedback mechanism that makes the loop recursive.

**Optimality**
The framework uses "optimal" to mean: the state toward which the IP is converging given the system's current constraints and information. It is always relative, never absolute. "Most optimal and ever-more-optimal" (MOEMO) is the canonical phrasing: MOEMOI does not claim any system reaches a fixed optimum, only that it moves toward one recursively.

**Life Operating System (LOS)**
The practitioner implementation of MOEMOI. The LOS is the set of practices, tools, and habits through which an individual runs the IP intentionally in their daily life. The LOS is free, voluntary, and decentralised; it is the product form of the framework.

**CoLive**
The product name for the LOS as a guided onboarding programme. The CoLive Starter Pack (included in this repository) is an 8-week introduction.

## Claims register terms

**Claims register**
A machine-readable list of 136 claims, each with: a unique identifier (C1, C2, ...), a formal statement, a confidence score (0.0 to 1.0), an epistemic status tier, and a provenance field. The register makes the framework's commitments explicit, sortable, and falsifiable.

**Confidence score**
A number from 0.0 to 1.0 representing the strength of evidence and argument currently supporting the claim. These are structured epistemic grades, not Bayesian probabilities. A score of 0.78 means "well-supported working claim, not yet tested via pre-registered comparative trial."

**Epistemic status tiers** (four canonical tiers plus one administrative status):

- **HYPOTHESIS:** The claim is plausible and internally consistent but lacks external empirical support. It is listed because it is load-bearing for the framework's architecture and needs to be tested.
- **CANDIDATE:** Supported by external evidence (literature convergence, observed pattern) but not yet formally adopted. Reviewers may disagree; the claim warrants scrutiny.
- **ADOPTED:** High-confidence claim (typically confidence >= 0.72) supported by multiple independent evidence streams. The framework commits to this claim for operational purposes. Adoption can be reversed if disconfirming evidence arrives.
- **ARCHIVED:** A claim that was formerly in the register but has been superseded, refuted, or absorbed into another claim. Archived claims are retained for audit trail purposes and must not be cited as active evidence.
- **HELD:** A claim that has been assessed as CANDIDATE or ADOPTED by the agent process but is pending explicit human review before promotion. Not yet active evidence.

## Architecture terms

**Void taxonomy**
A classification of the five deficit-type motivational drivers (epistemic void, conditional void, competence void, relational void, autonomy void) plus one generative drive. Each void represents a type of gap that motivates IP-running. The taxonomy is grounded in self-determination theory, curiosity research (Loewenstein 1994), and Maslow's hierarchy.

**Collective Effervescence (CE)**
A term from Durkheim: the feeling of social energy and shared purpose generated by collective synchronised activity. Used in MOEMOI as a named mechanism for why group IP-running (in communities, organisations) can amplify individual IP scores. Relevant to the multi-agent extension (Section 6).

**BRSI**
Bounded Recursive Self-Improvement. The framework's term for the IP applied to itself: the system most-optimally-and-ever-more-optimally optimises its own process of optimisation. "Bounded" because unconstrained recursive self-improvement is computationally unreachable; the IP operates within thermodynamic, cognitive, and resource constraints.

**CODEACCI**
An acronym for the proposed trajectory of capability amplification in intelligent systems: Complexity, Organisation, Depth, Efficiency, Abstraction, Compression, Convergence, and Integration. CODEACCI is labelled a "hedged trend" in the framework; it describes an observed pattern in historical capability growth, not a guaranteed trajectory. See C43 (CANDIDATE) for the confidence hedge.

**Multi-agent IP signal layer**
The extension of the IP to networks of intelligent agents (biological, artificial, institutional). The signal layer is a communication architecture in which each agent's IP outputs become Experience-layer inputs for other agents in the network. See Section 6 and the Federation Design Specification.
